# Carry card — five questions before submitting a name

## 1. Did I read the actual symbols?

**P = phosphorus. K = potassium.** A lowercase letter is part of its element symbol: Br is bromine. CO and Co are different.

## 2. Which naming model applies to this example?

**Ionic:** identify the complete cations and anions; balance their charges.  
**Molecular:** preserve the given atom counts; translate them with prefixes.

Nonmetal/nonmetal is a useful clue in these binary molecular examples. It is not the only rule: ammonium salts can be ionic without a metal; BF₃ and B₂H₆ use the covalent route here.

## 3. Did I do that model’s arithmetic?

**Neutral ionic formula:**

`whole-ion count × charge each`, added across the ions, must equal zero.

- 2 NH₄⁺ + 1 S²⁻ → **(NH₄)₂S** as the formula, not a balanced reaction equation.
- 2 K⁺ + 1 SO₄²⁻ → **K₂SO₄**.
- 1 Mg²⁺ + 2 ClO₃⁻ → **Mg(ClO₃)₂**.

**Molecular prefix name:**

1 mono · 2 di · 3 tri · 4 tetra · 5 penta · 6 hexa · 7 hepta · 8 octa · 9 nona · 10 deca.

- **Cl₂O:** dichlorine monoxide.
- **N₂O₅:** dinitrogen pentoxide.
- **PBr₃:** phosphorus tribromide.

Do not charge-balance or reduce a given molecular formula into a different formula.

## 4. What does the Roman numeral describe?

**The state/charge of one metal ion in this Stock-naming model—not the number of metal atoms.**

NiS: sulfide is −2, so Ni is +2 → **nickel(II) sulfide**.

## 5. Did I check both requested fields?

**Correct name/formula does not automatically certify the type label.** Check each independently.

Keep a known ion intact. Preserve peroxide’s O₂ group. Do not let hydride alone decide bonding. When a recognized common name differs from the requested prefix convention, identify the convention rather than pretending the chemistry disappeared.

### One sentence to keep

> Read the badge, choose the model, check the bookkeeping, then name what you actually have.
