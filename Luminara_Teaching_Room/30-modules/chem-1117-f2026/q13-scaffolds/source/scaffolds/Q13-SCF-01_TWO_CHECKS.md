# Two checks, one answer

**Scaffold ID:** Q13-SCF-01  
**Primary anchors:** CR-328, CR-330, CR-338  
**Related:** CR-306, CR-308, CR-342; L06; MAT-12, MAT-15  
**Time:** about 7 minutes  
**Model change:** “A row is either right or wrong” → “The identity, name/formula, and type can be checked separately.”

## 1. Teaching scene — two windows at the ticket desk

“Seatbelts everyone.”

Ms. Luminara parked the bus inside a station with two ticket windows. One was labeled **WHAT IS IT CALLED?** The other read **WHICH NAMING RULES ARE WE USING?**

A card passed through the first window:

**carbon disulfide → CS₂**

The clerk stamped it **MATCHES**.

The second window received **ionic**. Its light stayed amber.

**Natalie:** “But the formula worked. I thought that meant I had the row.”

Ms. Luminara covered the amber light with her hand, leaving the correct formula visible.

**Ms. Luminara:** “You have this part. We are not taking it away.”

**Henry:** “CS₂ says one carbon and two sulfur atoms. The name says the same thing.”

**Jackson:** “The second window asks what kind of compound we are naming, not whether the counts match.”

Ms. Luminara opened two display cases. One showed a molecular carbon–sulfur representation; the other showed positive and negative ion tokens for a familiar salt. The tokens were teaching models, not literal photographs.

**Natalie:** “Carbon and sulfur are nonmetals. In this worksheet case, the molecular route makes sense. The di- is reading the two sulfurs, not a charge of two.”

**Ms. Luminara:** “Exactly. Keep CS₂. Change the type to covalent.”

Kàra carried another card over: **BF₃ — boron trifluoride**.

**Kàra:** “Boron sits beside the staircase. That made me suspicious.”

**Ástríðr:** “Suspicious of the shortcut, or suspicious of the molecule?”

Kàra considered that.

**Kàra:** “The shortcut. Calling every metalloid a metal would make the map simpler, but the map would be lying.”

**Ms. Luminara:** “For this known boron-halide example, use molecular naming. Three fluorines: trifluoride. We do not manufacture a free B³⁺ ion merely to make the chart tidy.”

Carlos approached the two windows with one ticket.

**Carlos:** “Could I receive one large stamp saying ‘approximately chemistry’?”

**Jeff:** “No. That is how the bus gets classified as luggage.”

### Slow Turn

**Natalie:** “So I can correctly translate a name and still select the wrong type. That does not mean I need to rebuild the formula.”

**Ms. Luminara:** “Precisely. Locate the unresolved question before changing the answer.”

The clerk uncovered the amber light. Natalie changed only the type. The first stamp remained.

## 2. The conceptual tool

For the introductory cases in this activity:

| Question | Ionic naming route | Molecular naming route |
|---|---|---|
| What are the named constituents? | Cations and anions | Elements with specified atom counts |
| How is a formula constructed from a name? | Balance complete-ion charges | Read the prefixes as counts |
| Does neutrality settle the classification? | No; the whole salt is neutral | No; a neutral molecule is also neutral |
| What is the type label here? | Ionic | Covalent |

Metal/nonmetal patterns are clues, not a universal bonding proof. Recognized ammonium salts use ionic naming without a metal. Boron halides here use molecular naming. Unknown formulas need context; the application should be able to say “not established by this reference.”

## 3. Guided representation

Display one formula card with two independent status lines:

```text
Carbon disulfide
Formula: CS₂        Matches the named atom counts
Type: ionic         Revisit this field only
```

Provide carbon and sulfur periodic-table cards. Ask: “Which route applies to this example?” After the learner chooses molecular, connect **molecular naming** to the worksheet’s **covalent** type label. Do not imply that every covalent material is a discrete molecular substance.

Then give P₂O₃ with its already-constructed name, diphosphorus trioxide. Only classify the given exercise. Do not change its formula or claim that the worksheet proves a particular real structure.

## 4. Fading and return

First encounter: both windows and cards visible. Second: window labels visible, example explanation hidden. Return: existing field CR-330 or CR-328. Later: CR-316 or CR-340, independently, as related comparisons.

Suggested reflection: “Which part of the row did I preserve, and why?” This is optional self-review, not another test.

## 5. Codex hook

**Offer when:** a native type check disagrees while the paired name/formula agrees.  
**Do not infer:** that all naming knowledge failed or that the learner does not know valence.  
**Feedback template:** “The {name/formula} is correct. Let’s check only whether this example uses ionic or molecular naming.”  
**Success:** the learner revisits the type without losing the correct text or its native status.
