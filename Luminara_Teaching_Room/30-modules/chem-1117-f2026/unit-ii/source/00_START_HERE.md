## A 10–15 minute starting route

Use this as a small first session, not a completion deadline. You do not need to read the full practice catalogue in order.

1. **About 2 minutes:** Open [01_ions_formulas_naming.md](01_ions_formulas_naming.md) at **Station 0 Read the species before the name**. Try N³⁻, NH₄⁺, and N₂ from the Station 0 practice; say what the charge or subscript means
2. **About 3 minutes:** Read **Station 3 Build a neutral formula and audit it**. Attempt Q7l: combine ammonium and carbonate. Explain what the outside subscript counts before revealing the worked example
3. **About 3 minutes:** Try Q7c, then Q8c: combine Cu²⁺ with PO₄³⁻ and name the result. Use only the first matching hint if stuck; return to your answer before opening another
4. **About 2 minutes:** Fill one self-correction row from [03_SESSION_AND_FEEDBACK.md](03_SESSION_AND_FEEDBACK.md). Keep the first answer and state the rule that changed
5. **If you have a few minutes left:** Open [02_lewis_geometry.md](02_lewis_geometry.md) at **Lesson 1 An electron ledger comes before a picture** and try LG01. Stop after the budget expression if that is enough today

On your next visit, retry the item you corrected before opening its answer. If charge or ion recall was the sticking point, use Station 1 or 2 first. If formulas were comfortable, continue with the electron-ledger lesson. The long prompt and hint banks are lookup references, not assigned reading.

## Teaching intent

“Before we name it, let’s decide what kind of thing we’re looking at. Then we’ll give each symbol one job.”

The learner has a physics-teaching background. Build from conservation, counting, and models without assuming that chemistry vocabulary or ion names are already automatic. Use a warm adult-to-adult voice. Invite predictions, explanations, small revisions, and later transfer. Do not score speed or punish a broken streak. Any nursing connection is optional context; there are no medication or dosing exercises here.

Keep the worksheet numbering visible. Start with the immediate bottleneck: identify the species → distinguish charge and subscript → preserve a polyatomic ion → balance a formula → choose a naming system. Then build electron budgets and molecular geometry.
