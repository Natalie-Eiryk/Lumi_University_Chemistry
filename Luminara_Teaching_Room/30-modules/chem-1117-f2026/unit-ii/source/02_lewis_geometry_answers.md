# Lewis structures and geometry answer key

Use one answer after an attempt, then close the key and reconstruct the reasoning. These are chemistry solutions with explicit convention flags, not an instructor-supplied answer key. Original worksheet anchors are preserved. LP means one lone pair, or two nonbonding electrons. A bond line represents two electrons. Text skeletons below specify connectivity; the accompanying LP inventory is part of the complete Lewis structure.

## Q10 Octet exceptions

One defensible example set is H in HCl, with a duet; Be in simple monomeric BeCl₂, with four electrons around Be; B in BF₃, with six around B; and Al in monomeric AlCl₃, with six around Al. Alternatives include P in PCl₅, commonly drawn with ten electrons around P, and S in SF₆, drawn with twelve around S. These describe particular bonding situations; P in PBr₃ has an octet.

The four examples expected by the instructor cannot be recovered from the worksheet. Do not exact-match grade against this set. Ask which four examples the course uses and explain each with a specific compound. Do not add a pair merely to make an octet, and do not require d-orbital hybridization as an explanation of expanded-valence Lewis representations.

Audits:
- NCl₃: bonds 6 + Cl nonbonding 18 + N nonbonding 2 = 26
- HI: bond 2 + I nonbonding 6 = 8
- CHCl₃: bonds 8 + Cl nonbonding 18 = 26
- PBr₃: bonds 6 + Br nonbonding 18 + P nonbonding 2 = 26

Every atom in these structures has formal charge zero. H reaches a duet; all other atoms reach octets. Shared pairs count only bonding pairs, not every electron pair in the molecule. NCl₃ has 13 total electron pairs but only 3 shared pairs.

Drawing specifications can be rotated or reflected. The 2D placement of atoms on paper does not itself establish molecular shape. For example, NCl₃'s central N has tetrahedral electron geometry and trigonal pyramidal molecular geometry even when its three bonds are sketched as a flat T. Those geometry names are reinforcement beyond Q13's requested drawing.

## Q14a SiF4

Budget: 4 + 4(7) = 32 electrons. Draw central Si with four Si–F single bonds; each F has 3 LP and Si has none. Bonds 8 + terminal nonbonding 24 = 32. All formal charges are zero.

Central domains: 4 bonding + 0 LP = 4. Orbital/electron-domain geometry: tetrahedral. Molecular geometry: tetrahedral. Ideal angle: 109.5°. The names match because all four central regions lead to atoms.

## Q14b AlCl3

Budget: 3 + 3(7) = 24 electrons. In the monomer model, draw central Al with three Al–Cl single bonds; each Cl has 3 LP and Al has none. Bonds 6 + terminal nonbonding 18 = 24. All formal charges are zero. Al has six electrons around it, an incomplete octet.

Central domains: 3 bonding + 0 LP = 3. Both geometries: trigonal planar. Ideal monomer angle: 120°. Real AlCl₃ may associate into dimers; this answer is explicitly the simple monomer worksheet model. Do not add an Al lone pair: that would exceed the budget by two electrons.

## Q14c CO2

Budget: 4 + 2(6) = 16 electrons. Draw O=C=O, with 2 LP on each O and none on C. Bonds 8 + O nonbonding 8 = 16. All formal charges are zero. Carbon and both oxygens have octets.

Central domains: 2 bonding + 0 LP = 2. Each double bond is one domain. Both geometries: linear. Angle: 180°. There are four shared pairs in the molecule, but two bonding domains around C.

## Q14d SO3

Budget: 6 + 3(6) = 24 electrons. Confirm the class's Lewis convention before scoring the drawing.

Octet-preserving convention: Draw three equivalent resonance contributors. In each, S has one double bond to an O and single bonds to the other two O atoms. The double-bonded O has 2 LP and formal charge zero. Each single-bonded O has 3 LP and formal charge −1. S has no LP and formal charge +2. Bonds 8 + O nonbonding 16 = 24. The three contributors differ only in which O carries the double bond. Charge sum: +2 − 1 − 1 = 0. Do not give the neutral molecule an overall ionic charge.

Expanded-valence convention: Draw three S=O double bonds. Each O has 2 LP; S has none. Bonds 12 + O nonbonding 12 = 24. Every atom has formal charge zero. This picture places twelve electrons around S.

These are alternative Lewis representations; do not mix one convention's lone-pair inventory with the other's bonds. In both, central domains are 3 bonding + 0 LP = 3. Both geometries: trigonal planar. Angle: 120°. Geometry can be checked independently while Lewis-convention alignment is pending.

## Q14e CF2Cl2

Budget: 4 + 2(7) + 2(7) = 32 electrons. Draw C bonded singly to two F and two Cl atoms; each halogen has 3 LP and C has none. Bonds 8 + terminal nonbonding 24 = 32. All formal charges are zero.

Central domains: 4 bonding + 0 LP = 4. Both geometries: tetrahedral. Ideal reference angle: about 109.5°. Different terminal atoms mean actual bond angles need not all be identical; do not grade an exact measured angle that was neither supplied nor requested.

## Q14f NO2 minus

Budget: 5 + 2(6) + 1 = 18 electrons. The extra electron comes from the −1 overall charge.

Contributor A: [O=N–O]⁻. Double-bonded O has 2 LP and formal charge 0. N has 1 LP and formal charge 0. Single-bonded O has 3 LP and formal charge −1.

Contributor B: [O–N=O]⁻. The same LP and formal-charge rules follow the single- versus double-bond roles. N still has 1 LP. Place a resonance double-headed arrow between complete contributors. Brackets and the overall negative charge apply to the whole ion.

An annotated text drawing uses parenthetical labels rather than dot placement:

[ O(2 LP) = N(1 LP) – O(3 LP, −1) ]⁻  ↔  [ O(3 LP, −1) – N(1 LP) = O(2 LP) ]⁻

This is connectivity notation, not a drawing of the actual bond angle. The formal −1 on an O and the overall − outside brackets are two labels for different bookkeeping levels, not two added electrons.

Audit each contributor: bonds 6 + O nonbonding 10 + N nonbonding 2 = 18. N formal charge: 5 − 2 − 3 = 0. Double-bonded O: 6 − 4 − 2 = 0. Single-bonded O: 6 − 6 − 1 = −1. All atoms have octets. The two resonance contributors represent one hybrid with equivalent N–O bonds, not a molecule alternating between two fixed bond assignments.

Central domains: 2 bonding + 1 LP = 3. Orbital/electron-domain geometry: trigonal planar. Molecular geometry: bent (accept angular). Predicted O–N–O angle: less than 120°. The lone pair occupies a region but is not an atom included in the molecular shape name.

## Companion answers

- LG01: CHCl₃ has three Cl atoms and no overall charge; 26 electrons. NO₂⁻ has two O atoms and one extra electron; 18 electrons. Subscripts multiply atoms; the superscript changes the whole-species charge ledger.
- LG02: Use Q13a. The three N–Cl bonds use 6 electrons, Cl lone pairs 18, and N lone pair 2. One pair is shared per single bond.
- LG03: NH₃ has 5 + 3(1) = 8 electrons; three N–H bonds; 3 shared pairs; one N LP; no H LP. Each H has a duet. Replacing Cl with H removes terminal lone pairs, while N still has three bonds and one lone pair in this comparison.
- LG04: Three Al–Cl bonds plus nine Cl lone pairs already use 24 electrons. An added Al pair would give 26 electrons and fail the neutral-species ledger. Al's incomplete octet is allowed in the stated model. Moving a pair changes its location without changing the electron total.
- LG05: Four shared pairs, two bonding domains at C, zero C LP. A double bond has two pairs along one direction.
- LG06: Use Q14f. Essential reasoning: add one electron for negative charge, preserve 18 electrons while making a double bond, retain the N lone pair, include resonance, then distinguish three domains from two neighboring atoms.
- LG07: CO₂ has 16 electrons, no central LP, and two domains, so both geometries are linear. NO₂⁻ has 18 electrons, one central LP, and three domains, so its electron geometry is trigonal planar and molecular shape bent. Neighbor count alone is insufficient.
- LG08: NH₄⁺ is a polyatomic cation. Budget 5 + 4(1) − 1 = 8. Four N–H single bonds use all electrons; four shared pairs, zero N LP, four domains. Both geometries are tetrahedral. N formal charge is +1; H charges are zero. Draw brackets and an overall + charge. Positive charge subtracts; negative charge adds.
- LG09: NCl₃ has 26 electrons, not 26 shared pairs; its shared-pair count is 3. CO₂ has 2 central domains. NO₂⁻ molecular shape is bent. CF₂Cl₂ uses 109.5° as an ideal reference, not an exact promise for all actual angles. P satisfies an octet in PBr₃ but has an expanded-valence Lewis representation in PCl₅.

## Self grading without false certainty

For a complete correct drawing, require correct atoms/connectivity, electron total, every lone pair, appropriate shell checks, and charges. Require resonance when asked. Geometry answers must distinguish central bonding domains from shared pairs and electron geometry from molecular shape. A correct geometry word with an incorrect electron ledger deserves feedback and revision, not full reasoning credit.

Accept equivalent notation, rotation, reflection, and alternate harmless dot placement. Do not exact-text grade explanations. Leave Q10 and SO₃ instructor alignment open where the course preference is unknown. Q14's “orbital geometry” is interpreted provisionally as electron-domain geometry. Angle questions are companion reinforcement, not original worksheet requirements.
