# Sessions, companions, and feedback

This is a teaching interaction contract. It specifies desired behavior and learner copy; it does not assert that the campus currently implements it.

## The practice rhythm

**Predict → explain → check one thing → revise → reveal → transfer → return.**

Show one item at a time. Keep the source label, such as Q7l or Q14f, visible. Ask for a short reason alongside an answer, but allow the learner to continue when writing a reason is a barrier. Do not silently equate an empty explanation with poor understanding.

The first check should identify the earliest useful checkpoint rather than list every mistake. Preserve the first answer and each revision. A hint or answer reveal is learning activity, not a fresh independent correct attempt. Label assisted success honestly.

### Session A: numbers with different jobs

1. Ask: “What does the 3 mean in Fe³⁺? What does it mean in FeCl₃?” Record a prediction
2. Use the supplemental six-species diagnostic: N, N³⁻, NH₄⁺, (NH₄)₂CO₃, CO₂, N₂
3. Choose one weak checkpoint and its lesson; do not force every lesson on a learner who is ready
4. Work Q7l/Q8l (ammonium and carbonate) with staged help, then the Cu²⁺/PO₄³⁻ transfer
5. Try four independent items chosen from the original worksheet: one charge item, one ion recall item, one formula, and one name
6. Fill one correction row and retry the mistaken rule with a different species before returning to the original
7. Close: “What changed in your reasoning? What would you check first next time?”

### Session B: electrons to shape

1. Ask: “What changes in the electron budget when a species has a −1 charge?”
2. Model one Q11–13 molecule with the ledger, then fade one step at a time
3. Work NO₂⁻ through Q14f; distinguish electron geometry from molecular shape
4. Contrast CO₂: same number of attached oxygen atoms, different central lone-pair count
5. Try two electron-budget items and two geometry items independently
6. Correct one rule, then retry after another topic or on the next visit

Sessions are adjustable. Do not promise a fixed completion time or require a timer.

## Six reusable companions

Reuse the existing companion surfaces if present; these names describe roles, not new services or schemas.

| Companion | Learner-facing prompt | When to offer it | Exit check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two-check | “First check the whole species. Then check the job of each number.” | Classification or notation confusion | Learner identifies charge status and distinguishes superscript/subscript |
| Symbol | “Read the symbol aloud: element, count, charge.” | Lost charge sign, electron gain/loss, formula interpretation | Learner states what each visible mark means |
| Whole-ion | “Keep the named ion together. What does the outside number count?” | Polyatomic formulas and parentheses | Ion identity stays intact and smallest whole-number ion ratio is used |
| Roman | “The Roman numeral tells you the metal’s charge. What must it be here?” | Variable-charge cation naming | Charge follows from ion identity or neutrality, not atom count |
| Prefix | “Choose the molecular naming route. Let each prefix count atoms.” | Q15–16 molecular nomenclature | Correct prefix counts; molecular formula is not reduced |
| Chemical context | “Which model are we using, and where does the shortcut stop helping?” | OsO₄, AlCl₃, SO₃, octet exceptions, bond-type heuristics | Learner separates introductory prediction from an absolute rule |

Do not open all six companions at once. Offer the one associated with the failed checkpoint. A companion can be dismissed without losing the draft.

## Hint ladder and fading

For each item support three stages before the full explanation:

1. **Orient:** identify the relevant concept without providing its numerical result
2. **Focus:** name the specific check the learner should make
3. **Support:** provide a partial scaffold or one intermediate result, explicitly marked as help
4. **Reveal:** show the complete reasoning, not just a key

For a polyatomic formula: “What are the charges of the whole ions?” → “Find a common total positive and negative charge” → “Keep each ion intact while choosing how many copies you need.” For NO₂⁻: “How does negative charge change the budget?” → “Count central regions, not bond lines” → “Two bonding regions and one lone pair make three domains.” The third geometry hint discloses a result; record it as assisted.

Fade in response to demonstrated understanding: guided ledger → partially filled ledger → independent answer plus self-check → mixed transfer. Do not unlock harder material solely because an answer was revealed or copied.

## Feedback library

Use specific, nonjudgmental language. Acknowledge the valid part when it helps. Always offer an actionable next step.

| Error/checkpoint | Suggested feedback |
|---|---|
| Species type | “There’s an overall charge on this species. Start with the ion branch, even though it contains more than one element.” |
| Charge sign | “Electrons carry negative charge. If the atom loses one, which sign should the remaining species have?” |
| Ion recall | “You have the right kind of building block. Check this ion’s formula and charge in the reference drawer, then close it and retry.” |
| Charge balance | “Add the charges from every ion copy. Does the total reach zero?” |
| Unreduced ionic ratio | “This balances, but the ion ratio can be smaller. Reduce the number of whole ions, keeping each ion’s internal formula unchanged.” |
| Lost ion identity | “Keep this polyatomic ion’s internal subscripts unchanged. Only the number of whole copies may change.” |
| Parentheses | “The outside subscript needs to multiply the whole polyatomic ion. Group that ion before adding the outside count.” |
| Roman numeral | “The numeral describes the metal’s charge. Use the anion charges to infer it; don’t copy the formula’s subscript.” |
| Naming route | “Decide whether this task uses ionic names or molecular prefixes before choosing the ending and numbers.” |
| Electron budget | “Count the valence electrons from every atom, then adjust for the overall charge. Compare that budget with the electrons drawn.” |
| Lewis structure | “Audit every bond and lone pair. Hydrogen needs two electrons; check the model and octet expectations for the other atoms.” |
| Domain count | “A single, double, or triple bond occupies one direction from the central atom. Count regions, then include central lone pairs.” |
| Shape vs electron geometry | “Your domain arrangement includes the lone pair. The molecular-shape name describes only the atomic positions.” |
| Convention-sensitive answer | “This is a chemically defensible model or name. The worksheet does not specify your instructor’s preferred convention, so keep it for class alignment rather than marking it wrong.” |

A correct formula with an incorrect explanation is not “fully understood.” Say what is correct, identify the reasoning issue, and ask for a short revised explanation. An equivalent notation or accepted synonym should not incur a cosmetic penalty.

## Acceptance and accessibility behavior

- Accept unambiguous plain-text notation and Unicode subscripts/superscripts; normalize only after interpretation
- Keep ionic charge distinct from atom count. Do not accept a chemically different formula because formatting looks similar
- Treat definitions and drawings with concept checklists or learner self-assessment when dependable semantic/drawing evaluation is unavailable
- Reference drawer starts closed during recall, remains available, and records that it was used
- Keep answer explanations collapsed until requested or the learner chooses to finish the check
- Preserve entered text, working, and focus through hints and feedback; never erase a draft merely to display an error
- Announce feedback through the supported accessible surface; label fields and maintain a predictable keyboard order
- If feedback focus is moved, do so intentionally after explicit checking and preserve a clear way back to the field
- Do not rely on color alone; keep formulas legible in narrow layouts and at increased text size
- “Not yet checked,” “needs revision,” “correct with help,” and “independent correct” are distinct states
- Draft persistence, successful save, and submission are distinct states. Never claim saved until the authorized store confirms it
