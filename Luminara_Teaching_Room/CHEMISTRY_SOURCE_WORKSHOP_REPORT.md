## 2. Source findings that shaped this build

### Process before answer

`Q4Dim_Analysis.pdf`, PDF page 1, explicitly says:

> I have given you the answers, I am grading your work. All problems must be solved utilizing Dimensional Analysis. DO NOT google your conversion factors.

This supports making the visible unit route central. The original page remains beside the learner's setup, and the fence-post bench inspects entered arithmetic and literal unit cancellation. It does not turn one correct number into a declaration that the required method has been demonstrated.

The four-page `Dim_fen.pdf` and earlier `dim_analysis.pdf` are both annotated. They are treated as different attempts/representations, **not** a blank exercise and a certified answer key. Q4's separately printed answers are identified as printed answers, not confused with the handwritten layer.

### Representation is not the same as reporting

The scientific-notation Q3 sheet, its annotated counterpart, the significant-digit scan, and the two measurement records support comparing the quantity, how it is written, its units, and the reporting decision. The Q3 instruction separates a calculator result from the rounded result. The Number lens consequently checks numerical equivalence but leaves warranted significant figures and rounding as a separate learner response. It does not strip meaningful notation and pretend a numeric parser can infer measurement precision.

`sig figs 2.pdf` and `sig figs 2units.pdf` provide a useful comparison of records with and without units. The actual scales for those recorded measurements are not supplied, so the app does not certify estimated digits from an unseen instrument.

### An average can be rebuilt from its contributions

`Q6Isotopes.pdf` includes the fictional Unk-33/35/37 model with shares 28.5%, 55.6%, and 15.9%. The abundance bench displays each entered mass-times-share contribution and their unrounded sum. Using the worksheet's integer-mass model yields 34.748. That is arithmetic on the specified model, **not a statement about measured exact isotope masses or the correct rounded submission**. Changed entries are labeled learner what-ifs. A total other than 100% is flagged instead of silently normalized.

### Three representations need a shared reference

The blank Q9, two annotated Q9 versions, and annotated electron-configuration tutorial support a desk with separate fields for shorthand configuration, orbital notation, Lewis representation, and the connection between them. The nine element choices come from Q9. Uncertain handwritten rows—especially transition-element configurations and dot conventions—are not promoted to official rules. This is a comparison/annotation desk, not an automatic ground-state configuration checker.

### A lab observation should remain an observation

The safety sheets and polymer handout remain original sources. The new guide asks the learner to connect a rule to its rationale, distinguish a prediction from an observation, and identify which comparison could support a claim. It does not invent observations, sign safety attestations, state that a missing video was completed, or produce a finished laboratory report. Broad causal or safety statements in the handout remain attributed to that handout rather than being independently certified.

The textbook exercise pages contain some health-related calculations. They are retained as course material; the app does not turn them into clinical dosing advice.

## 3. The teaching structure

| Workshop | Topic / framing | Guided stops |
|---|---|---:|
| The particles keep their story | Matter and change · Chapter 1 picture questions | 5 |
| A number changes clothes | Q3 · value, notation, and reporting | 5 |
| The unit returns to the measurement | Recorded readings, significant digits, and missing instrument views | 5 |
| Let the units show the route | Dimensional analysis · preserve the fence-post method | 5 |
| One element, a weighted crowd | Q6 · identity bookkeeping and relative abundance | 5 |
| Three views of one row | Configuration → orbital notation → Lewis dots | 5 |
| Name the question before drawing the arrow | Periodicity notes · process, direction, and scope | 5 |
| A rule needs a reason | Safety narrative and cartoon · discussion preparation | 5 |
| An observation is not yet an explanation | Polymers · source comparison and your existing data | 5 |

Every route follows **Notice → Explore → Name → Change → Return**. Each stop has a source page, an open question, an authored Ms. Luminara opening and follow-through, and three optional small-step prompts. Nothing requires an answer before moving on. No free-text meaning, lab conclusion, or student worth is graded.

This carries forward the existing design commitment: context remains visible at the transition into writing. Source studio and Bench tools have independently scrolling source and response panes. Paired-version links and page selectors update the source area without replacing the writing field. The learner can enlarge the source or open it in a full-view dialog. On narrow screens the upper source pane and lower writing pane remain visible together. Quiet desk removes authored guidance, not the original page.

For example, the fence-post route opens:

> The fence posts are not decoration around an answer. They make each conversion visible. Your archive includes an earlier working page, a more structured fence-post version, and Q4 with printed answers. That gives us a place to study the route rather than race to the destination.

The instructor's wording is labeled separately from this guidance. The gallery also retains page-specific learner margins so moving between pages does not merge different annotations into one box.

### Four bounded tools

1. **Number lens:** accepts numeric strings rather than executable expressions; compares the starting value with a coefficient times ten to an integer power. Arithmetic, ordinary normalized form, and significant-figure reporting are explicitly different questions. Bad numeric input produces an editable diagnostic, not executable code.
2. **Fence-post bench:** up to eight factors, each with numerator/denominator values and units. Shows unmatched literal units and integer powers separately from the numeric result. Does not silently treat differently named units as identical or validate a conversion fact. A zero denominator is rejected. Rounding is recorded separately.
3. **Abundance table:** three rows from the worksheet's fictional model, visible individual contributions, total-share check, and an unrounded weighted sum. No silent correction of a non-100% total. Edits do not alter the source page.
4. **Three-view desk:** per-element notes on configuration, orbital notation, Lewis notation, connections, and questions. No invented answer key or universal transition-element convention.

The existing pilot's six routes, eighteen response stops, twelve definition/chart associations, eleven change examples, and notebook remain available. Their content file is byte-for-byte unchanged. The new spaces are added ahead of the original tabs rather than replacing their content or IDs.
