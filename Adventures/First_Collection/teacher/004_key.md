# Teacher key for the Compound Naming Station

Story ID: luminara.adventure.004

## Purpose and use

Assess the separation of compound category, composition, and oxidation state. A correct answer with a shaky explanation invites one chosen transfer attempt, not an endless adaptive loop. Hints, reference use, and pausing have no penalty. Preserve the original response and record revisions separately. These are human-readable rubrics, not claims of automated semantic grading.

## Question 1 Worked answer

CuCl: one chloride contributes −1, so one copper contributes +1. Name: copper(I) chloride.

CuCl₂: two chlorides contribute −2, so one copper contributes +2. Name: copper(II) chloride.

The Roman numeral specifies copper's oxidation state, matching the ion charge in these examples. The subscript after Cl counts chlorides in the formula-unit ratio. Neither formula has a displayed subscript after Cu, so each ratio contains one copper.

Rubric, 4 points: both names correct, 1 point each; correct charge accounting, 1; correctly distinguishes oxidation state from subscript count, 1. If “copper two chloride” is spoken, ask what two means before deciding whether the reasoning is wrong.

First helpful prompt: “Keep the copper count and copper charge in separate spaces. What charge do all the chlorides contribute?”

## Question 2 Worked answer

Two NH₄⁺ ions give +2; one CO₃²⁻ gives −2. The neutral formula is (NH₄)₂CO₃ and the name is ammonium carbonate. The outside 2 multiplies every atom inside the ammonium group. Composition per formula unit: N 2, H 8, C 1, O 3, totaling 14 atoms. This is a counting description of the ratio, not a claim of separate molecular units.

It contains oppositely charged polyatomic ions despite consisting only of nonmetal elements. The bonds within those ions are covalent; the compound is ionic overall.

Rubric, 4 points: intact-ion formula and neutrality, 1; conventional name, 1; ionic-versus-internal-covalent explanation, 1; all atom counts, 1. An expanded atom list can earn the counting point but cannot replace the requested ion-preserving formula. Do not award a wrong formula merely because its total atom count matches.

First helpful prompt: “If you add a second ammonium ion, which atoms come with it?”

## Question 3 Worked answer

MgCl₂: magnesium chloride. The supplied Mg²⁺ and two Cl⁻ balance charge. In this introductory convention magnesium's fixed common ion charge makes a Roman numeral unnecessary; explicit magnesium(II) still expresses the correct charge. Do not classify it as chemically nonsensical.

PCl₃: phosphorus trichloride. Each molecule contains one phosphorus and three chlorine atoms.

SF₆: sulfur hexafluoride. Each molecule contains one sulfur and six fluorine atoms.

A molecular prefix di is not used before chloride in the requested ionic name magnesium chloride. Tri and hexa in the molecular names specify atoms per molecule, not charge.

Rubric, 4 points: three correct names, 1 point each; explicit category/number distinction, 1. Handle spelling separately from composition. A correct count with a spelling error is not evidence that the learner confused charge and count.

First helpful prompt: “The problem has already supplied the category. Which naming system goes with that category?”

## Question 4 Worked answer

H₂O₂ states that each molecule contains two hydrogen atoms and two oxygen atoms. HO describes its simplest whole-number atom ratio, so it is the empirical formula in this context. It does not preserve the molecule's atom count. The requested molecular formula must remain H₂O₂.

N₂O₄ has two nitrogen and four oxygen atoms per molecule; NO₂ has one nitrogen and two oxygen atoms per molecule. Their simplest ratios match, but their molecular compositions differ. No conclusion about chemical equilibrium or reaction conditions is needed or licensed by reducing the notation.

Rubric, 4 points: original molecular count, 1; correct meaning of empirical formula, 1; rejects replacement for the stated task, 1; correct N₂O₄/NO₂ distinction, 1.

First helpful prompt: “Are you describing a ratio or the contents of one molecule?”

## Interpreting evidence

A suggested milestone is a worked example plus one independently explained transfer, chosen with the learner. The optional 16-point total is a local scoring convenience, not a mastery diagnosis. Report category choice, charge balance, formula composition, and explanation separately when that is more useful. Offer revealed worked answers directly if requested; do not require failed attempts to unlock them.
