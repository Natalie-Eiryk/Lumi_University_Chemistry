# Teacher guide and key for The Address With No Row

Story ID: luminara.adventure.001

## Purpose and prerequisites

Learners distinguish a family clue from a unique element identification, separate partial from complete electron information, and keep identity tied to proton count. Prerequisites: whole-number addition and a willingness to use a labeled table. This story supplies the needed configuration vocabulary; memorized configurations are not prerequisites.

## Reasoned key

- **q01:** Insufficient for a single element. Both O and S satisfy the supplied scope. Period 2 chooses O; period 3 chooses S. An atomic number or other genuinely distinguishing observation also works. Naming one possible atom without excluding the other does not establish uniqueness.
- **q02:** Chloride, Cl⁻. Seventeen protons fixes chlorine; one extra electron gives charge −1 in elementary-charge units. It is not argon. The original neutral-atom condition excludes this ion.
- **q03:** Five electrons occupy the 3p subshell. The complete total is 2 + 2 + 6 + 2 + 5 = 17. Neutrality makes the proton count equal seventeen. Do not identify atomic number from electron count without that condition.
- **q04:** Accept any scientifically accurate statement that similarities in outer structure support recurring chemical patterns without requiring identical properties. The metaphor must be labeled and bounded. This is reasoning review, not a keyword item.

## Feedback and self review

Use “established,” “partly established,” and “not yet shown” for three visible criteria: preserves the stated scope; accounts for all candidates/counts; explains why new evidence narrows the result. No total score is needed. A correct element with unjustified certainty needs a reasoning repair, not a lecture about intelligence. Help use is a study condition, not a penalty. For q04, assess distinctions and limits, not personal agreement or eloquence.

If only chlorine was chosen, try: “Chlorine fits. What excludes fluorine before the period arrives?” If the learner has already retained both candidates, acknowledge that repair and proceed. Do not repeatedly correct the original response.

## Source and model notes

The narrative and practice are original fiction. Atomic number, group/period organization, and the displayed ground-state configurations were checked against [IUPAC periodic table](https://iupac.org/what-we-do/periodic-table-of-elements/), [NIST fluorine](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/fluorinetable1.htm), and [NIST chlorine](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/chlorinetable1.htm), accessed 7 October 2026. These NIST pages are used for atomic numbers and electronic states, not as a source of current atomic-weight values. The author computes the sums. The table/neighborhood and attention/well comparisons are explicitly metaphors.
