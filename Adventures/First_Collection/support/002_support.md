# Support for The Bracket Luggage Office

Story ID: luminara.adventure.002

## Words in this setting

- **Noble-gas core notation** abbreviates an occupied configuration using a noble-gas symbol in brackets. It does not put a noble-gas atom inside another atom.
- **Occupancy** means how many electrons a subshell contains in the stated configuration.
- **Ion** means a particle with net electric charge. A monatomic ion has one nucleus; its ordinary chemical ion formation changes electrons, not its proton identity.
- **Isoelectronic** in the atomic comparison here means the same electron count and matching ground-state configurations. It does not imply the same nucleus, size, energy, or element.
- **Core** here refers to the inner-electron shorthand. Earth's core and a computer processing core are different senses.

## Help before an attempt

1. Expand the bracket: [He] stands for 1s²; [Ne] for 1s² 2s² 2p⁶; [Ar] for 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶.
2. Add every electron occupancy, including those hidden by the bracket.
3. Check both the total and the correct occupancy for the stated species. A right total alone does not prove a ground-state configuration is correct.
4. If asked for a neutral atom's nearest earlier noble gas, choose the closest noble gas with lower atomic number and keep all remaining occupied terms. At a noble gas itself, the task can request the preceding core; a self-label is not the learning objective.

## Charge ledger

For a monatomic ion, charge in elementary-charge units = number of protons − number of electrons. Na⁺ has 11 − 10 = +1. Ordinary electron removal leaves the eleven protons unchanged. A surroundings column keeps track of the electron outside the illustrated atom. The ledger does not establish a real reaction's energy balance or spontaneity.

## Optional deeper excursion

Orbitals and subshells belong to a quantum model. The luggage shelves illustrate accounting, not spatial compartments or classical electron tracks. The neutral first-twenty configuration sequence is a limited teaching scope. Do not turn it into a universal “remove the last-written electron” rule for all ions and transition metals.

## Optional diagram description

Three columns read “protons,” “electrons,” and “net charge.” Neutral Na is 11, 11, 0. Na⁺ is 11, 10, +1. Neutral Ne is 10, 10, 0. Connect Na⁺ and Ne with a line labeled “same electron count,” and distinguish their proton columns. No animation is needed.
