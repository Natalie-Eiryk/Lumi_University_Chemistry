# Support for The City Without Couples

Story ID: luminara.adventure.003

## Words in this setting

- **Ion:** a species with net electric charge. **Cation:** positive ion. **Anion:** negative ion.
- **Polyatomic ion:** a charged group containing multiple atoms, such as NH₄⁺. Its internal covalent bonds and overall ionic charge describe different things.
- **Molecule:** here, a discrete neutral bonded entity, such as N₂ or CO₂. A molecule need not contain more than one element.
- **Compound:** a substance containing more than one element chemically combined. N₂ is elemental, not a compound; CO₂ is a compound.
- **Lattice:** an extended repeating structural arrangement. The salt-crystal story concerns ordinary solid NaCl, not every state or condition of sodium chloride.
- **Formula unit:** the represented composition ratio for an ionic compound. It is not evidence of independent molecular pairs in the crystal.

## Help ladder for a formula

1. Keep the ions intact. Read each entire charge, including the sign.
2. Choose whole-number quantities that make total charge zero.
3. Use the simplest such ratio of ion copies. Do not reduce the subscripts inside a polyatomic ion.
4. Use parentheses when multiple copies of a polyatomic ion need an outside subscript: two NH₄⁺ with one CO₃²⁻ gives (NH₄)₂CO₃.

## A worked contrast

N₂ is a neutral molecule of one element, with covalent bonding. NH₄⁺ is a polyatomic ion with internal covalent bonds. The neutral solid ammonium carbonate is an ionic compound composed of ammonium and carbonate ions. The absence of a metal does not make every substance molecular.

## Optional deeper excursion

“Ionic” and “covalent” are useful descriptions with idealized endpoints; real bonds can have mixed character. Electron transfer bookkeeping is not itself the attractive force holding a lattice together. The pair-ribbon story corrects that misconception without claiming to give a full quantum theory of solids.

## Optional diagram description

Place a sodium ion at the center of a coordinate sketch. Its six nearest chloride neighbors lie in the positive and negative x, y, and z directions. Label each ion and charge in text. This is a local environment in the ordinary rock-salt structure, not a six-to-one formula or isolated seven-ion molecule. Separately show two intact ammonium tiles and one carbonate tile; their charges sum to zero.
