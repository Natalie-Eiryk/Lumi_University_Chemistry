## A reference receipt

- Amount of substance n uses the unit mole, symbol mol
- Particle number N counts the specified entities
- N = n × N_A, with N_A exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹
- Mass m is related by m = n × M, where M is the appropriate molar mass
- State the entity: neon atoms, O₂ molecules, oxygen atoms within those molecules, or another specified entity

For an ionic solid, one mole of formula units refers to specified composition units in the lattice, not separate molecules.

The exact Avogadro constant does not make measured sample amounts or approximate molar masses exact.

## A smaller picture

Imagine three O₂ molecules. They contain six oxygen atoms. Scaling the same relationship up gives one mole of O₂ molecules containing two moles of oxygen atoms. Those atoms need not be free atoms; the words “within the molecules” preserve the meaning.

## A weighted average scaffold

For the story's artificial isotope model:

Average atomic mass = (0.80 × 10 u) + (0.20 × 11 u) = 10.2 u.

The fractions describe proportions of atoms, not proportions of mass. They sum to one. An answer between 10 and 11 and closer to 10 is plausible because the lighter model isotope is more abundant.

## Optional deeper distinction

An average atomic mass can be expressed in u. Relative atomic mass, also called atomic weight, is the ratio to the atomic mass constant and is dimensionless. Molar mass has units such as g/mol. These are related quantities, not interchangeable unit labels.

Real isotope masses are measured values and generally are not whole numbers in u. Integer neutron counts do not require integer masses in u. The 10 u and 11 u values are explicitly invented simplifications. A decimal average does not describe an individual nucleus with fractional neutrons.

No real gas, isotope separation, or nuclear construction is being performed; these are fictional displays. See 004_005_sources.md for validation links.
