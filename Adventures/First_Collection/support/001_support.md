# Support for The Address With No Row

Open any support before attempting a question. Reading the story requires no quiz. Stop after any scene, read the explanation directly, or return to the story without answering.

## Words in this setting

- **Atomic number** counts protons and identifies an element. It is not an atomic mass.
- **Neutral** means total positive and negative charge balance. In these atoms the proton and electron counts are equal.
- **Ground state** is the lowest-energy state; the configurations in this story concern ground-state neutral atoms.
- **Period** means a horizontal row of the periodic table. **Group** means a numbered column. A time period is a different use of the word.
- **Valence electrons**, for the neutral main-group examples here, are the electrons in the outermost occupied principal level. Do not export this simple counting rule without checking its scope.
- **Subshell** is an electron-state category such as 2s or 2p. The superscript states its electron occupancy. The initial number and letter are not electron counts.

## A help ladder

1. Orient: Is the clue a whole configuration or only an outer pattern?
2. Focus: Did it specify a value for n, a period, or an atomic number?
3. Scaffold: Compare fluorine's 2s² 2p⁵ with chlorine's 3s² 3p⁵. They fit the same ns² np⁵ pattern but different rows.
4. Full explanation: The first clue leaves both F and Cl possible within the neutral first-twenty scope. Adding period 3 selects Cl. Its full configuration totals seventeen electrons, matching seventeen protons because it is neutral.

## Optional deeper excursion

The periodic table is useful because electron structures and chemical patterns recur. It is not a claim that all group members are identical. Their energies, sizes, and specific reactions can differ. The neighborhood is a metaphor for classification; atoms do not literally occupy streets, and electrons do not follow the bus's paths.
