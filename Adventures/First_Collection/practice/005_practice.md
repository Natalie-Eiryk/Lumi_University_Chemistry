# Transfer questions for the Mole Counting Station

Story ID: luminara.adventure.005

These questions are separate from the story. Choose an untimed, reference-aided attempt unless another mode is agreed beforehand. A calculator, a drawn collection, a spoken explanation, or a written calculation are all appropriate. Keep the entity and unit beside each answer.

Reference constant: N_A = exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

## Question 1 A new molecule on the receipt

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q01

In a counting thought experiment, a collection contains exactly 0.25 mol of N₂O₄ molecules.

How many N₂O₄ molecules does it contain? How many moles of nitrogen atoms and of oxygen atoms are within those molecules? How many moles of atoms are present in total? Explain why a molecule count and an atom count are different here.

## Question 2 Equal amounts and unequal masses

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q02

Compare 2.00 mol of neon atoms with 2.00 mol of O₂ molecules. Use the supplied approximate molar masses, 20.18 g/mol for neon and 32.00 g/mol for O₂.

Do the samples contain equal numbers of their specified entities? Calculate their approximate masses. Does the O₂ sample contain 2.00 mol or 4.00 mol of oxygen atoms within its molecules? Explain your choices.

## Question 3 A different artificial isotope mixture

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q03

For a deliberately simplified artificial example, assign two isotopes of one element masses of 24 u and 26 u. The sample contains 75% of its atoms as the 24 u isotope and 25% as the 26 u isotope.

Find the average atomic mass. Explain why that result does not mean every atom has that mass or contains fractional neutrons. Check whether your answer lies in a reasonable place between the two isotope masses. These values are not a real element's isotope data.

## Question 4 Reconstructing a mixture

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q04

Use the same artificial 24 u and 26 u isotope model, but with a different sample. Its average atomic mass is 24.8 u.

What fraction and percentage of its atoms are the 26 u isotope? Show a calculation, a picture, or a clear verbal method. Would adding more 26 u atoms while keeping all original atoms raise, lower, or preserve the average?

## Optional reflection

Choose one phrase to put on a confusing laboratory receipt: “Specify the entity,” “Keep the units,” or “Ask what the average describes.” Explain your choice if you wish. This is not scored.
