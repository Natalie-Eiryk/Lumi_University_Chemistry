## luminara.adventure.002.card01

**Definition.** What does [Ne] represent in neutral sodium’s [Ne] 3s¹ configuration?

**Answer:** It abbreviates 1s² 2s² 2p⁶, accounting for ten core electrons. It is not a neon atom inside sodium.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Accept the expanded configuration or a precise ten-electron core description; retain the distinction between notation and a physical extra atom.

## luminara.adventure.002.card02

**Definition.** What does an occupancy check ask after a configuration’s electron total has been counted?

**Answer:** Whether those electrons occupy the appropriate stated subshells for the specified species and state.

**Reasoning to recognize:** A correct total alone does not pass this check. A ground-state check needs a suitable verified configuration.

## luminara.adventure.002.card03

**Definition.** In the chapter’s Na⁺ and Ne comparison, what does isoelectronic mean?

**Answer:** They have the same electron count and matching ground-state electronic configurations, while their nuclei identify different elements.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Accept same electron configuration or same ten-electron inventory with the stated ground-state comparison. Do not infer identical size or energies.

## luminara.adventure.002.card04

**Definition.** What makes the sodium ion Na⁺ a cation?

**Answer:** Its net electric charge is positive; Na⁺ has one more proton than electron.

**Reasoning to recognize:** The defining feature is positive net charge, not being a metal or having only one atom.

## luminara.adventure.002.card05

**Contrast.** How do [He] 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² and [Ne] 3s² differ as descriptions of neutral magnesium?

**Answer:** They expand to the same electron configuration; the neon-core form is the compact version requested by a nearest-earlier-noble-gas convention.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Distinguish correct inventory from requested formatting. The longer helium-core expression is not a lost-electron error.

**Story:** [A longer answer that tells the truth](STORY.md#ch02-scene-06); [Two numbers above the line](STORY.md#ch02-scene-11)

## luminara.adventure.002.card06

**Contrast.** Why can [Ar] 3d² pass an electron-count check for neutral Ca yet fail its ground-state check?

**Answer:** The argon core plus two electrons totals 20, but verified neutral ground-state calcium is [Ar] 4s², not [Ar] 3d².

**Reasoning to recognize:** State that total and placement are different checks. Do not turn this example into a universal transition-metal removal rule.

**Contrast.** What remains the same when neutral Na becomes Na⁺ by electron removal in the chapter’s bookkeeping model?

**Answer:** Its nucleus still has 11 protons, so its elemental identity remains sodium; the electron inventory changes.

**Reasoning to recognize:** An unchanged proton count/element explanation is enough. Ion formation is not a nuclear change here.

## luminara.adventure.002.card08

**Contrast.** How does a charge ledger differ from an explanation of whether sodium ionization occurs spontaneously?

**Answer:** The ledger accounts for charges and the removed electron. Spontaneity needs energetic and environmental information that the count alone does not supply.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Credit the boundary between conservation/bookkeeping and a physical process’s feasibility. No advanced thermodynamic calculation is expected.

## luminara.adventure.002.card10

**Application.** How many electrons are missing from [He] 3s² if it is proposed for neutral magnesium, Z = 12?

**Answer:** Eight. The written inventory is 2 + 2 = 4, whereas neutral magnesium needs 12; 2s² 2p⁶ is missing.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Credit the difference 12 − 4 = 8 or identification of the omitted eight-electron block.

**Story:** [Eight missing from the label](STORY.md#ch02-scene-04); [Two numbers above the line](STORY.md#ch02-scene-11)

**Method transfer:** Scene 4 demonstrates the missing 2s² 2p⁶ block in sodium; scene 11 supplies neutral magnesium’s twelve-electron inventory. The eight-electron omission transfers to magnesium; q01 explicitly tests that case.

## luminara.adventure.002.card11

**Application.** What net charge does a monatomic species with 12 protons and 10 electrons have?

**Answer:** +2 in elementary-charge units, because 12 − 10 = +2.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Accept 2+, +2, or a labeled balance drawing. Removing negative charge makes the remaining balance more positive.

**Story:** [Two numbers above the line](STORY.md#ch02-scene-11)
