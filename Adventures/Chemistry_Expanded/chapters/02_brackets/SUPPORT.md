## Contextual glossary

**Atomic number.** The number of protons in an atomic nucleus. Eleven protons identifies sodium whether a particular monatomic species has eleven, ten, or another number of electrons. Electron count identifies atomic number only with appropriate additional information, such as a known neutral atom. See [scene 9](STORY.md#ch02-scene-09).

**Neutral atom.** An atom with equal numbers of protons and electrons, so their electrical charges sum to zero. Neutral does not mean the individual particles have no charge. See [scene 2](STORY.md#ch02-scene-02).

**Monatomic ion.** A charged species with one atomic nucleus. In ordinary electron-loss or electron-gain ion formation, the proton count remains unchanged. Na⁺ has eleven protons and ten electrons; Mg²⁺ has twelve protons and ten electrons. These are not neon atoms. See [scenes 9–11](STORY.md#ch02-scene-09).

**Cation and anion.** A cation carries a net positive charge; an anion carries a net negative charge. Removing electrons from a neutral atom makes its remaining charge positive. Adding electrons makes it negative. The main story models cations; this definition does not imply that every element forms every imagined ion stably under ordinary conditions.

**Net charge.** For the atomic examples here, net charge in units of the positive elementary charge is proton count minus electron count. Twelve minus ten gives +2. The charge on the whole species and the number of particles are different quantities. See [scene 11](STORY.md#ch02-scene-11).

**Electron configuration.** A description of the occupied subshells of an atom or ion. The terms encode more than a total electron count. A matching total does not by itself establish the correct ground-state arrangement. See [scenes 14–15](STORY.md#ch02-scene-14).

**Ground state.** The lowest-energy state of the specified atom or ion. A label claiming a ground-state configuration must be checked against an appropriate reference or supported model. The story's [Ar] 3d² proposal has twenty electrons but is not neutral calcium's verified ground-state configuration. It is not rejected merely because d can never contain two electrons. See [scene 15](STORY.md#ch02-scene-15).

**Shell.** A grouping of atomic orbitals with the same principal quantum number, n. The leading 2 in 2p⁶ is a shell label, not an occupancy of two electrons. Shells are not literal rings or rigid surfaces. See [scenes 2 and 14](STORY.md#ch02-scene-02).

**Subshell.** A set of orbitals with the same n and angular-momentum quantum number; s, p, and d designate subshell types. A shell can include multiple subshells. For example, the third shell includes 3s, 3p, and 3d. An unoccupied subshell's existence does not mean it must be filled before all higher-numbered shells. See [scene 14](STORY.md#ch02-scene-14).

**Orbital.** A spatial wavefunction used for one electron in the atomic model; its squared magnitude gives a probability density. It does not by itself specify spin. The familiar pictures represent aspects of probability, not a classical electron trajectory. In the orbital approximation used for introductory configurations, a spatial orbital can be occupied by at most two electrons, with opposite spin projections. Those electrons do not have the same complete set of quantum numbers. Spin is an intrinsic quantum property, not a literal spinning-ball mechanism. See [scene 16](STORY.md#ch02-scene-16).

**Occupancy and capacity.** Occupancy is the number of electrons actually assigned to a subshell in a specified configuration. Capacity is the maximum allowed for that type of subshell: s, two; p, six; d, ten. Passing a capacity check does not prove a configuration is the ground state. The third shell contains eight electrons in the shown Ar, K, and Ca ground states, but eight is not that shell's maximum capacity. See [scenes 14–16](STORY.md#ch02-scene-14).

**Noble-gas core notation.** A noble-gas symbol in square brackets abbreviates its ground-state electron configuration. It is a configuration reference, not a noble-gas atom or gas sample lodged inside another atom. Rewriting an unchanged configuration in shorthand does not physically remove electrons or drive an electronic transition. See [scene 3](STORY.md#ch02-scene-03).

**Nearest earlier noble gas.** In the neutral-atom convention practiced here, the noble gas with the greatest atomic number below the target's atomic number. The remainder of the actual configuration stays outside the bracket. At neon itself, an explicit preceding-core request uses helium: [He] 2s² 2p⁶. A bare [Ne] is a meaningful ten-electron reference but does not demonstrate that requested preceding-core expansion. See [scene 7](STORY.md#ch02-scene-07).

**Isoelectronic.** In this chapter's atomic comparisons, having the same number of electrons; the examples Ne and Na⁺ also have matching written ground-state occupancies. Isoelectronic does not mean the same element, nuclear charge, size, energy, or every physical property. Do not extend the simplified atomic definition into a blanket statement about arbitrary molecules. See [scenes 9 and 12](STORY.md#ch02-scene-09).

**System boundary and surroundings.** The boundary identifies what is included in a particular account. When an electron leaves the atom being counted, it must be represented outside that atomic boundary if the larger charge ledger is to remain complete. A complete system need not be neutral: conservation requires unchanged total charge when none crosses its outer boundary, rather than a total of zero. Moving a counter demonstrates accounting, not the complete physical transfer mechanism. See [scene 10](STORY.md#ch02-scene-10).

**Ionization energy.** Energy required to remove an electron from the specified isolated gas-phase atom or ion in the stated initial state. The story uses qualitative ground-state comparisons. A charge-balanced equation does not supply the energy balance or establish spontaneity of a real process. See [scene 12](STORY.md#ch02-scene-12).

## Open the three cases

- [He] represents 1s², totaling 2 electrons.
- [Ne] represents 1s² 2s² 2p⁶, totaling 10 electrons.
- [Ar] represents 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶, totaling 18 electrons.

The case never represents “whatever inner electrons happen to be needed.” Its expansion is fixed. Retain every occupied term not actually replaced by it.

## Selected verified neutral ground states

- Li, atomic number 3: 1s² 2s¹ = [He] 2s¹
- Ne, atomic number 10: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶; with an explicit preceding-core request, [He] 2s² 2p⁶
- Na, atomic number 11: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹ = [Ne] 3s¹
- Mg, atomic number 12: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² = [Ne] 3s²
- Ar, atomic number 18: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶; with an explicit preceding-core request, [Ne] 3s² 3p⁶
- K, atomic number 19: [Ar] 4s¹
- Ca, atomic number 20: [Ar] 4s²

The equal signs here compare representations of the same supplied configuration; they do not denote a chemical reaction. See NIST links in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

## Give each symbol one job

- In 3s², the leading 3 identifies the shell, s identifies the subshell, and the raised 2 counts electrons in that subshell.
- In Mg²⁺, Mg identifies magnesium and 2+ gives the net charge of one magnesium ion.
- In [Ne], square brackets mark a configuration abbreviation in this context. Brackets have other chemical uses in other contexts; do not globally replace every bracketed expression with a noble-gas core.
- A chemical-formula subscript counts atoms or groups in its specified formula context; it is not the electron-occupancy superscript used here. A coefficient before a species counts whole specified entities or proportional amounts in an equation. Those topics receive fuller treatment in later adventures.
- Unambiguous accessible forms such as 3s^2, Mg^2+, and e^- may be used when superscripts are unavailable. Preserve letter case, charge signs, and the role of each number. Ask for clarification when plain text is ambiguous.
- Some scientific references omit a subshell's superscript when its occupancy is one, as in 3s instead of 3s¹. This chapter normally prints the one for clarity. A deliberate conventional omission and a damaged required mark are different cases; scene 18 concerns the latter.

## A short check that does not overpromise

1. Read the stated species, state, and requested representation.
2. Expand each noble-gas bracket.
3. Add occupancies, including the hidden core.
4. Compare occupied subshells with the appropriate ground-state reference if ground state is claimed.
5. Use proton count for elemental identity and protons minus electrons for net charge when relevant.
6. Separately check the requested notation convention.
7. When a particle transfer is modeled, keep the surroundings account and identify what remains unspecified about mechanism or energy.

Use only the checks relevant to the task. “Not enough information” and “outside this model” are legitimate outcomes. These checks are learning supports, not a certified automated grader.
