# Teacher key for The Bracket Luggage Office

Story ID: luminara.adventure.002

This is a reasoned self-study companion, not an instructor-certified answer key or an implemented semantic grader. Help may be used before an attempt. Accept accurate ordinary-language explanations, clear labeled diagrams, and unambiguous accessible notation. Evaluate the response actually given; do not repeat a correction the learner has already made.

## Core review distinctions

Keep these checks separate and apply only those relevant to the prompt:

- Is the abbreviation expanded faithfully?
- Does the total fit the stated species?
- Does the occupancy agree with a verified ground-state reference when ground state is claimed?
- Are element identity and net charge identified from the appropriate particle counts?
- Is the requested notation convention met?
- Does a modeled transfer retain a surroundings account?
- Has the explanation stayed within what the model establishes?

Correct arithmetic does not establish every other claim. Conversely, a formatting-convention mismatch does not erase correct arithmetic or a faithful expansion. “Not enough information,” “not yet shown,” and “outside the supplied model” are not synonyms for incorrect.

## luminara.adventure.002.q01

**Worked answer.** [He] 3s² expands to 1s² 3s² and represents 2 + 2 = 4 electrons. Relative to the supplied twelve-electron magnesium configuration, it omits the eight electrons in 2s² 2p⁶. [He] 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² expands completely and totals 2 + 2 + 6 + 2 = 12. [Ne] 3s² also expands completely and totals 10 + 2 = 12; it meets the specified nearest-earlier-core convention.

**Feedback focus.** The longer helium form is chemically consistent with the supplied configuration. It is not the preferred nearest-core form. The first expression has an inventory omission, a different error. The preserved question's phrase “lose electrons” is interpreted as losing terms from the description; rewriting symbols does not physically ionize magnesium.

**Model limit.** Counts are necessary but should be checked alongside the supplied occupancy. No orbital-energy derivation is needed. See [scenes 4 and 6](STORY.md#ch02-scene-04); sources N4 and T1 in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

## luminara.adventure.002.q02

**Worked answer.** Twelve protons identify magnesium. Net charge is 12 − 10 = +2 in elementary-charge units, so the ion is Mg²⁺. Neon would have ten protons. Equal electron count alone does not make the species the same element or give them identical charge, energy, size, or all other properties.

**Feedback focus.** Separate correct elemental identity from correct sign. If a learner writes magnesium but gives −2, preserve the identity and revisit the subtraction of negative charge. If they write neon, compare the proton columns directly.

**Model limit.** These counts do not establish a real formation pathway, abundance, or reaction energy. See [scene 11](STORY.md#ch02-scene-11); N2, N4, T3.

## luminara.adventure.002.q03

**Worked answer.** [Ar] 3d² contains 18 + 2 = 20 electrons, so the total-count check passes. The supplied verified neutral-calcium ground-state configuration is [Ar] 4s², so the last two electrons are assigned to the wrong subshell for the stated ground-state task. A d subshell can contain two electrons; the failure is not simply an impossible capacity.

**Example feedback.** “Your total accounts for all twenty electrons. Now compare the final occupied subshell with the supplied ground-state reference: neutral calcium has 4s² beyond [Ar], so that is the part to revise.”

**Model limit.** Do not claim that every configuration other than the ground state is physically impossible. Do not universalize a 4s/3d energy ordering across atoms and ions. See [scene 15](STORY.md#ch02-scene-15); N7, T1, T2.

## luminara.adventure.002.q04

**Worked answer.** [He] 2s² 2p⁶. The explicit instruction uses the immediately preceding noble-gas core, so neon's own label is not the requested demonstration. A bare [Ne] remains a meaningful reference to the ten-electron neon configuration; it must not be described as ten missing electrons. Changing the representation does not change the physical species.

**Feedback focus.** Explain the task convention rather than presenting it as a law that forbids all other uses of [Ne]. See [scene 7](STORY.md#ch02-scene-07); N2, T1, P01.

## luminara.adventure.002.q05

**Worked answer.** Opening [Ne] gives 1s² 2s² 2p⁶. Retaining 3s² 3p⁶ 4s¹ reproduces the full supplied potassium configuration. The total is 10 + 2 + 6 + 1 = 19. Since argon is the nearest noble gas with lower atomic number than potassium, the requested form is [Ar] 4s¹.

**Feedback focus.** The clerk's longer neon-core expression preserves both count and occupancy. Its revision concerns the requested compact convention, not missing electrons. One should not subtract the exposed 3s² 3p⁶ from potassium physically when folding them into [Ar].

**Model limit.** This is neutral potassium's ground state; it is not a universal ordering recipe for all species. See [scene 14](STORY.md#ch02-scene-14); N5, N6, T1.

## luminara.adventure.002.q06

**Worked answer.** A is neutral Ne: ten protons, ten electrons, charge 0. B is Na⁺: eleven protons, ten electrons, charge +1. C is Mg²⁺: twelve protons, ten electrons, charge +2. They share an electron count of ten. They do not share elemental identity or net charge. The common count does not establish identical size, ionization energy, or every property.

**Feedback focus.** An answer naming the shared count and one defensible nonidentity is sufficient. Learners need not memorize additional ion properties or numerical radii.

**Model limit.** Do not infer that any ten-electron molecular species must have this same simple atomic occupancy structure. See [scenes 9–11](STORY.md#ch02-scene-09); N2, N3, N4, T3.

## luminara.adventure.002.q07

**Worked answer.** The given argon ground state has 2 + 6 = 8 electrons in the third shell: 3s² 3p⁶. That is its occupancy in this state. The supplied possible third-shell subshell capacities total 2 + 6 + 10 = 18. The 3d subshell exists but is unoccupied in the shown ground-state configuration.

**Feedback focus.** Preserve the accurate occupied count of eight while correcting the inference about maximum capacity. The shell number and the occupancy superscripts have distinct jobs.

**Model limit.** Capacity does not dictate the order of filling in multielectron ground states. The existence of 3d does not make [Ar] 3d¹ neutral potassium's ground state. See [scenes 14 and 16](STORY.md#ch02-scene-14); N5, N6, T1, T2.

## luminara.adventure.002.q08

**Worked answer.** In 3s², 3 specifies the principal shell, s the subshell type, and superscript 2 the number of electrons occupying that subshell. In Mg²⁺, Mg identifies magnesium and superscript 2+ gives the net charge, +2, of one magnesium ion. Mg₂ has a subscript count rather than that charge notation. Silently substituting it would change the meaning and would not identify the intended magnesium cation.

**Feedback focus.** Do not globally strip superscripts, signs, or letter case. Mg^2+ is acceptable accessible notation. A bare Mg2+ typed by a learner may require clarification rather than automatic rejection if intent is not otherwise clear.

**Model limit.** This question does not ask whether a particular neutral magnesium cluster exists or how to name it. Its objective is preserving the role of a symbol. See [scene 11](STORY.md#ch02-scene-11); T1, T3.

## luminara.adventure.002.q09

**Worked answer.** The remaining atom has three protons and two electrons: net charge 3 − 2 = +1. It is Li⁺. A charge-balanced representation is Li → Li⁺ + e⁻. Initially the atomic part is neutral. Afterward, the atomic part has charge +1 and the transferred electron in the surroundings has charge −1; together they retain the initial net charge 0.

**Feedback focus.** Keep the proton count fixed and put the departing electron on the products side. An answer using labeled before-and-after ledgers can demonstrate the same understanding as the equation.

**Model limit.** The equation alone does not identify the physical ionization mechanism, energy source, or conditions. Removing an electron from an isolated gas-phase ground-state lithium atom requires energy input; charge balance alone does not establish a spontaneous reaction. The total is zero because this example begins with a neutral atom and no other modeled charge. Charge conservation also applies to systems with nonzero total charge. See [scenes 10 and 12](STORY.md#ch02-scene-10); N1, T3.

## luminara.adventure.002.q10

**Worked answer.** The two species have different nuclear charges: Ne has ten protons, Na⁺ eleven. Their common electron count and matching configuration labels do not specify identical interactions or electronic energies. Consequently, equality of the next ionization energies does not follow. Modern NIST records in fact give different values, but a numerical comparison is not required by the question.

**Feedback focus.** “Same count, different proton number and interactions” is a sufficient conceptual response. Do not require a quantum derivation or an unsupplied general trend prediction.

**Model limit.** Avoid saying that any one simple factor universally determines all ionization-energy comparisons. See [scene 12](STORY.md#ch02-scene-12); N2, N3, T1.

## luminara.adventure.002.q12

**Worked answer.**

- A: 18 + 2 = 20; 4s² respects s capacity; it matches the supplied ground state.
- B: 18 + 2 = 20; 3d² respects d capacity; it does not match neutral calcium's supplied ground state. This is the requested counterexample to “right total plus allowed capacity proves ground state.”
- C: 18 + 3 = 21, so it fails the neutral-calcium count. 4s³ also exceeds the s-subshell capacity of two. It does not match the ground state.

**Feedback focus.** Keep the three criteria distinct rather than giving a single unexplained mark. If a learner correctly flags C's over-capacity, invite them also to check its total, without erasing the valid first observation.

**Model limit.** A hypothetical string that violates a subshell capacity is different from an allowed-capacity but nonground assignment. No assignment of an excited-state energy or stability is requested. See [scenes 15–16](STORY.md#ch02-scene-15); N7, T1, T2.

[He] 3s¹ would be the expression that omits the middle eight electrons; it is not the expression you wrote.”

**Model limit.** The preserved original question set uses “lose electrons” informally for representational omission; precise feedback should not imply that writing a shorter expression ionizes an atom. See [scenes 6 and 18](STORY.md#ch02-scene-06); N3, T1, P01.

## luminara.adventure.002.q14

**Worked answer.** A matching expansion establishes fidelity to the supplied configuration. A total of twenty establishes the electron count represented, not the correct ground-state occupancy or element identity without other information. Eleven protons establishes sodium as the element, but not its electron count or charge. Spectral evidence of a constituent supports presence under the conditions and interpretation of the observation; it does not automatically quantify the amount from relative line intensity alone in the cited historical example.

**One sufficient extension.** “Twenty electrons is compatible with neutral calcium, but without the proton or charge information it does not by itself identify calcium; it also does not determine where all twenty occupy the ground-state configuration.”

**Historical limit.** The 1898 Ramsay–Travers letter is documentary evidence. Dialogue in the adventure is invented and supplies no historical quotation or testimony. The modern nucleus/configuration briefing is expressly later knowledge given to the fictional visitor. See [scenes 8, 17, and 19](STORY.md#ch02-scene-08); H3, N3, N7, T1.

## Feedback and access notes

- A learner can use the reference before failing. Requesting a demonstration is a valid route.
- A concise answer that explains the relevant distinction is sufficient. Do not demand every model limit for every response.
- Read signs, subscripts, superscripts, and symbol case as meaningful; accessible ASCII is acceptable when unambiguous. A deliberately omitted occupancy of one is conventional in some references, including NIST. Do not treat that convention as an electron-count error; distinguish it from the damaged print in scene 18's explicit-occupancy job.
- Do not treat speed, polished prose, elaborate personal reflection, or participation in the quantum excursion as evidence of chemistry competence.
- Review a repaired answer as the current answer. Keep old mistakes only when they still help the learner understand a change.
- The practice is original private-study reinforcement. It is not a completed course submission, a current instructor rubric, or a software grading specification.
