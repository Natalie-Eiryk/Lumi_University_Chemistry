## NIST atomic data

The linked National Institute of Standards and Technology *Handbook of Basic Atomic Spectroscopic Data* pages were opened for the stated atomic numbers and ground-state configurations. Their older atomic-weight columns are not used here. In these tables, spectral stage I means neutral and II singly ionized; those Roman numerals are not the compound-naming convention taught in later adventures.

**N1 — Lithium.** [NIST Atomic Data for Lithium](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/lithiumtable1.htm). Atomic number 3; Li I ground configuration 1s² 2s¹; Li II configuration 1s²; positive ionization-energy requirement. Supports scene 7 and q09's supplied example.

**N2 — Neon.** [NIST Atomic Data for Neon](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/neontable1.htm). Atomic number 10; neutral ground state 1s² 2s² 2p⁶. Supports the neon core, preceding-core example, neutral-neon comparison, and qualitative ionization-energy distinction.

**N3 — Sodium.** [NIST Atomic Data for Sodium](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/sodiumtable1.htm). Atomic number 11; neutral ground state 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹; singly ionized ground state 1s² 2s² 2p⁶. Supports the central shorthand and Na⁺ examples. The page also supports the distinction between the energy to ionize Na and the next ionization of Na⁺; the story gives no numerical energy calculation.

**N4 — Magnesium.** [NIST Atomic Data for Magnesium](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/magnesiumtable1.htm). Atomic number 12; neutral ground state 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s². Used for the neutral magnesium reference. Mg²⁺ charge identity follows from the stipulated twelve-proton/ten-electron ledger; this particular NIST page's Mg II row denotes Mg⁺, not Mg²⁺, and is not misused as a direct Mg²⁺ entry.

**N5 — Argon.** [NIST Atomic Data for Argon](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/argontable1.htm). Atomic number 18; neutral ground state 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶. Supports the [Ar] expansion and eighteen-electron core.

**N6 — Potassium.** [NIST Atomic Data for Potassium](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/potassiumtable1.htm). Atomic number 19; neutral ground state [Ar] 4s¹. Supports scene 14 and q05. The office does not derive the configuration by sorting shell numbers.

**N7 — Calcium.** [NIST Atomic Data for Calcium](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/calciumtable1.htm). Atomic number 20; neutral ground state [Ar] 4s². Supports the count-versus-occupancy comparison with the authored incorrect proposal [Ar] 3d². The latter is not presented as a verified excited state or assigned an energy.

**N8 — Helium.** [NIST Atomic Data for Helium](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/heliumtable1.htm). Atomic number 2 and neutral ground configuration 1s². Independently checks the two-electron core used throughout the missing-eight and preceding-core examples.

**N9 — Shells, subshells, and Pauli constraints.** NIST, [Atomic States, Shells, and Configurations](https://www.nist.gov/pml/atomic-spectroscopy-compendium-basic-ideas-notation-data-and-formulas/atomic-spectroscopy-10). Directly read. Supports the quantum-number distinctions, s/p/d capacities, the third shell's capacity of eighteen, and the distinction between a spatial orbital and a complete one-electron state including spin. Its many-electron discussion explicitly uses an approximation. It also records the conventional omission of an occupancy superscript of one. These checks support scene 16, the orbital glossary, and scene 18's distinction between a deliberate notation convention and a damaged required print.

**N10 — Reading atomic data.** NIST, [Ground States and Ionization Energies help](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/ASD/Html/iehelp.html). Directly read. Supports atomic number, spectral-stage versus ion-charge notation, the atomic isoelectronic-sequence convention, [Ne]/[Ar] expansions, ionization energy, and the optional omission of an occupancy of one. Its description of ground-configuration labels also cautions that a configuration label need not express the entire exact many-electron state. The story uses selected verified ground-state records without claiming that a count alone derives them.
