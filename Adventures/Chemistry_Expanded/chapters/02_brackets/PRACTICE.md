# Optional practice for The Bracket Luggage Office

Story ID: luminara.adventure.002

Use references and reveal support whenever useful. Explain a checkpoint; do not rely on recognizing a familiar string. All practice is optional; untimed, spoken, typed, and labeled-diagram responses are welcome. Original questions q01–q04 are preserved verbatim. New questions q05–q14 are authored reinforcement, not copied course assessment items. Progressive hints follow the questions so you can choose how much help to use.

## luminara.adventure.002.q01

Neutral magnesium has atomic number 12 and ground-state configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s². Compare [He] 3s², [He] 2s² 2p⁶ 3s², and [Ne] 3s². Which lose electrons, which expand correctly, and which meets a request for the nearest earlier noble-gas form?

## luminara.adventure.002.q02

An ion has 12 protons and 10 electrons. Identify it using Mg = 12 and Ne = 10. State its charge and one reason it is not neon. Does the ten-electron count by itself describe all its properties?

## luminara.adventure.002.q03

A learner proposes [Ar] 3d² for neutral calcium, atomic number 20. The supplied verified ground-state form is [Ar] 4s². Both count twenty electrons. What does the first check establish, and what second check fails? Respond without calling the learner careless.

## luminara.adventure.002.q04

Your task says “write neon using the immediately preceding noble-gas core.” The full configuration is 1s² 2s² 2p⁶. Give the requested notation and explain why this is a convention-sensitive task, not a physical change in neon.

## luminara.adventure.002.q05

A record states neutral potassium, atomic number 19, ground-state configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s¹. A clerk writes [Ne] 3s² 3p⁶ 4s¹. Expand the bracket and check the total. Then rewrite the record using the nearest earlier noble-gas core. Explain which part of the clerk's work was already correct.

## luminara.adventure.002.q06

Three records all contain ten electrons. Record A has ten protons; B has eleven; C has twelve. Using Ne = 10, Na = 11, and Mg = 12, identify each species and its net charge. Name one shared property established by the records and one property that the shared electron count does not establish as identical.

## luminara.adventure.002.q07

A learner reads argon's ground-state configuration, 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶, and says, “The third shell can hold only eight electrons.” Explain what the configuration actually establishes and what the learner has inferred beyond it. The third shell includes 3s, 3p, and 3d; their capacities are 2, 6, and 10 electrons, respectively.

## luminara.adventure.002.q08

Explain the job of each number or sign in 3s² and Mg²⁺. Would silently changing Mg²⁺ into Mg₂ preserve the meaning? A complete chemical naming discussion is not required; identify the count-versus-charge difference.

## luminara.adventure.002.q09

Start with one neutral lithium atom: three protons and three electrons. Model one electron leaving the atom. State the resulting ion and its charge. Write a charge-balanced electron-loss equation and describe the atom and surroundings ledgers before and after. What important physical question does this bookkeeping alone leave unanswered?

## luminara.adventure.002.q10

A machine says, “Ne and Na⁺ each have ten electrons, so removing the next electron must require the same energy.” Both have the written ground-state configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶. Explain why the conclusion does not follow. You do not need numerical ionization energies or a quantitative quantum calculation.

## luminara.adventure.002.q11

Two job slips concern neon's configuration. Slip A says “write it with the immediately preceding noble-gas core.” Slip B says “state what the abbreviation [Ne] represents.” Give an appropriate answer to each. Explain why a different task can require a different representation without changing neon physically.

## luminara.adventure.002.q12

The verified neutral-calcium ground state is [Ar] 4s², with twenty electrons. Compare three proposals: A, [Ar] 4s²; B, [Ar] 3d²; C, [Ar] 4s³. The argon core represents eighteen electrons; s can hold at most two and d at most ten. For each proposal, separately discuss total count, subshell capacity, and agreement with the supplied ground state. Which proposal demonstrates that a correct total and allowed subshell capacity still do not establish the ground state?

## luminara.adventure.002.q13

A clerk receives [He] 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹ for a neutral-sodium nearest-earlier-core task and responds, “Incorrect. You lost eight electrons.” Write two or three sentences of more accurate feedback. Preserve what is valid, specify what the task still requires, and identify the separate expression that really would omit the middle eight electrons.

## luminara.adventure.002.q14

During the fictional visit, Ramsay asks what a measurement or check actually establishes. Match each observation to the limited conclusion it supports: a bracket expansion matches a supplied full configuration; an electron total equals twenty; a nucleus has eleven protons; an 1898 gas spectrum shows evidence of a constituent. Then choose one of those observations and describe an additional claim it cannot establish by itself. Do not treat the invented guest dialogue as historical testimony.

## Progressive hints

Take one hint, several, or the complete worked explanation in [TEACHER_KEY.md](TEACHER_KEY.md). Needing help does not invalidate an answer.

### q01 hints

1. Open [He] and [Ne] using [the reference cases](SUPPORT.md#open-the-three-cases).
2. Helium hides 2 electrons; neon hides 10. Add the terms outside each bracket.
3. Separate “expands to the full configuration” from “uses the closest earlier core.” Read “lose electrons” as an omission from the written inventory, not literal ionization by writing.

### q02 hints

1. Which particle count identifies an element?
2. Charge in elementary-charge units is protons minus electrons.
3. Twelve protons remain magnesium; the electron count alone does not specify the nucleus or all properties.

### q03 hints

1. Add 18 and 2 for both expressions.
2. Locate the subshell occupied by the last two electrons in each.
3. Keep the successful count in your feedback, then name the failed ground-state match.

### q04 hints

1. Does preceding mean the target itself?
2. Helium is the preceding noble gas for neon.
3. Replace only 1s² with [He] and retain the other occupied terms.

### q05 hints

1. [Ne] expands to ten electrons; add 2 + 6 + 1 outside it.
2. Argon's ground-state configuration includes 3s² 3p⁶ as well as the neon prefix.
3. The original clerk's expression can be valid while longer than the requested nearest-core form.

### q06 hints

1. Read each proton count before interpreting its electron total.
2. Compute 10 − 10, 11 − 10, and 12 − 10.
3. Same electron count does not mean same element, charge, or all physical properties.

### q07 hints

1. Distinguish “contains in this state” from “could accommodate.”
2. Identify the unoccupied third-shell subshell omitted from the displayed occupied terms.
3. The stated capacities total 18; the given argon configuration occupies only 8 in that shell.

### q08 hints

1. In 3s², separate the leading 3 from the raised 2.
2. In Mg²⁺, the plus sign is part of the net charge.
3. A subscript 2 indicates a count in a formula context and cannot silently replace a charge superscript.

### q09 hints

1. Keep three protons; reduce electrons from three to two.
2. Put the electron on the products side, outside the atomic system.
3. Compare total charge on both sides, then ask what supplied the energy for an actual process.

### q10 hints

1. Compare nuclear charges, not only electron totals.
2. A matching configuration label does not mean identical interactions or state energies.
3. The actual ionization-energy comparison requires evidence; the equality is not entailed by isoelectronic status.

### q11 hints

1. Slip A requests a preceding-core demonstration; Slip B asks for an expansion.
2. For A begin with [He]; for B write the full ten-electron configuration.
3. A description can change while the species stays in the same physical state.

### q12 hints

1. Totals are 18 + 2, 18 + 2, and 18 + 3.
2. Compare 4s³ with the supplied s capacity of two.
3. Proposal B respects the d capacity but disagrees with the ground-state reference.

### q13 hints

1. Expand the submitted longer form before evaluating the feedback.
2. The nearest earlier core for sodium is neon.
3. The missing-eight expression is [He] 3s¹, which is not what this learner submitted.

### q14 hints

1. Fidelity, total, elemental identity, and evidence of presence answer different questions.
2. Ask whether the observation supports occupancy, energy, amount, or physical mechanism as well.
3. One possible limit: a constituent's spectral evidence need not quantify its proportion from relative line intensity alone in the historical example.
