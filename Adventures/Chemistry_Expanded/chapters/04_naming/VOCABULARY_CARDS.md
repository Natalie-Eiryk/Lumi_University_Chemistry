## luminara.adventure.004.card01

**Definition.** What does III specify in the name iron(III) oxide?

**Answer:** Iron’s oxidation state is +3. In this supplied simple monatomic-ion model, that agrees with the Fe³⁺ ion charge.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Accept oxidation state plus three. Do not equate every oxidation state in all compounds with a localized physical ionic charge.

## luminara.adventure.004.card03

**Definition.** In FeCl₂, what does the subscript 2 after Cl specify?

**Answer:** Two chloride ions per iron ion in the conventional formula-unit composition ratio of this supplied ionic compound.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Count chloride in the ionic composition; it neither counts iron atoms nor states the iron oxidation state by itself.

## luminara.adventure.004.card04

**Definition.** What is the empirical formula of N₂O₄ intended to report?

**Answer:** Its simplest atom-number ratio, N:O = 1:2, written NO₂.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Explain the ratio purpose. An empirical formula does not state that an N₂O₄ molecule has only one N and two O atoms.

## luminara.adventure.004.card05

**Contrast.** How do the II in iron(II) chloride and the 2 in FeCl₂ perform different jobs?

**Answer:** II names iron’s +2 oxidation state; the subscript 2 gives the chloride count relative to iron in the formula-unit composition.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Keep formal oxidation state separate from the ion ratio. Both are related by neutrality, but they are not interchangeable symbols.

## luminara.adventure.004.card06

**Contrast.** Why does the chapter use magnesium chloride for ionic MgCl₂ but phosphorus trichloride for molecular PCl₃?

**Answer:** The supplied ionic naming route names ions without molecular count prefixes; the binary molecular route uses prefixes for atoms per molecule.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Identify the naming convention before interpreting the subscript. Magnesium(II) chloride is not chemically nonsensical; the numeral is unnecessary in the stated introductory convention. Broader compositional nomenclature also permits count prefixes for ionic compounds; a prefix alone does not prove molecular structure.

## luminara.adventure.004.card07

**Contrast.** Why may H₂O₂ have empirical formula HO while its molecular formula must remain H₂O₂?

**Answer:** HO gives a simplest ratio; H₂O₂ gives the actual two hydrogen and two oxygen atoms per molecule. The requested information is different.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Credit ratio versus molecule contents. Reducing the written formula does not preserve its molecular-composition statement.

## luminara.adventure.004.card08

**Contrast.** Why should known NH₄⁺ and SO₄²⁻ identities outrank the shortcut “no metal means molecular” for (NH₄)₂SO₄?

**Answer:** The species are verified ions, so the compound follows the ionic route even though all of its elements are nonmetals.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Use the identity/structure evidence as the stronger basis. A shortcut is a clue, not a universal classification law.

## luminara.adventure.004.card09

**Application.** For supplied simple ionic Fe₂O₃ with O²⁻, which Roman numeral belongs in iron’s name?

**Answer:** III: three oxide ions contribute −6, so two iron ions must contribute +6 in total, or +3 each.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Accept the charge equation 2q + 3(−2) = 0, a diagram, or clear verbal balancing. The 2 after Fe is not the Roman numeral.

**Story:** [The six that belonged to nobody alone](STORY.md#ch04-scene-04)

## luminara.adventure.004.card10

**Application.** For supplied simple ionic CuCl₂ with Cl⁻, which oxidation state belongs to copper?

**Answer:** +2, since two chloride ions contribute −2 and the one copper ion must balance them.

**Reasoning to recognize:** A neutral-charge sum is enough. The corresponding introductory name is copper(II) chloride, but naming spelling is not this card’s target.

**Application.** In the binary molecular name sulfur hexafluoride, how many F atoms belong to one molecule?

**Answer:** Six, because hexa- gives the fluorine count; the molecular formula is SF₆.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Accept six with a prefix explanation. This is atom count, not a fluorine charge.
