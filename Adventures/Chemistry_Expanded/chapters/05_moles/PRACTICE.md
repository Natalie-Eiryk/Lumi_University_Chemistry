# Transfer questions for the Mole Counting Station

Story ID: luminara.adventure.005

These questions are separate from the story. Choose an untimed, reference-aided attempt unless another mode is agreed beforehand. A calculator, a drawn collection, a spoken explanation, or a written calculation are all appropriate. Keep the entity and unit beside each answer.

Reference constant: N_A = exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

## Question 1 A new molecule on the receipt

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q01

In a counting thought experiment, a collection contains exactly 0.25 mol of N₂O₄ molecules.

How many N₂O₄ molecules does it contain? How many moles of nitrogen atoms and of oxygen atoms are within those molecules? How many moles of atoms are present in total? Explain why a molecule count and an atom count are different here.

## Question 2 Equal amounts and unequal masses

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q02

Compare 2.00 mol of neon atoms with 2.00 mol of O₂ molecules. Use the supplied approximate molar masses, 20.18 g/mol for neon and 32.00 g/mol for O₂.

Do the samples contain equal numbers of their specified entities? Calculate their approximate masses. Does the O₂ sample contain 2.00 mol or 4.00 mol of oxygen atoms within its molecules? Explain your choices.

## Question 3 A different artificial isotope mixture

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q03

For a deliberately simplified artificial example, assign two isotopes of one element masses of 24 u and 26 u. The sample contains 75% of its atoms as the 24 u isotope and 25% as the 26 u isotope.

Find the average atomic mass. Explain why that result does not mean every atom has that mass or contains fractional neutrons. Check whether your answer lies in a reasonable place between the two isotope masses. These values are not a real element's isotope data.

## Question 4 Reconstructing a mixture

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q04

Use the same artificial 24 u and 26 u isotope model, but with a different sample. Its average atomic mass is 24.8 u.

What fraction and percentage of its atoms are the 26 u isotope? Show a calculation, a picture, or a clear verbal method. Would adding more 26 u atoms while keeping all original atoms raise, lower, or preserve the average?

## Optional reflection

Choose one phrase to put on a confusing laboratory receipt: “Specify the entity,” “Keep the units,” or “Ask what the average describes.” Explain your choice if you wish. This is not scored.


## Optional hints for preserved Questions 1–4

The four original question texts and IDs above are unchanged. Choose any hint or the full key immediately; no attempt is required first.

- q01: Orient: distinguish one molecule from its constituent atoms. Focus: N₂O₄ contains two N and four O per molecule. Scaffold: multiply 0.25 mol molecules by each constituent count separately, then add only the atom amounts for the total atom question.
- q02: Orient: equal mol amounts imply equal counts of their named entities. Focus: use each substance’s own molar mass. Scaffold: m = nM; the O₂ atom amount is twice its molecular amount.
- q03: Orient: the supplied percentages describe atom counts. Focus: convert 75% and 25% to fractions. Scaffold: 0.75(24 u) + 0.25(26 u).
- q04: Orient: locate the mean between the two artificial masses. Focus: the mean is 0.8 u above the 24 u baseline; the full mass gap is 2 u. Scaffold: the heavy atom fraction is the fraction of that gap traversed.

# New expansion questions

These ten original questions extend the preserved set. They are not course-sheet items or a compulsory sequence. All isotope values below are deliberately artificial teaching data.

## Question 5 A bare mole label

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q05

A receipt says “1 mol” and nothing else. A clerk claims it must describe 1 mol of molecules with a mass of 1 g. Identify the missing information and both unsupported claims. Give two examples of appropriate specified entities.

Hints: Orient: a mole is an amount unit. Focus: is every substance molecular, and is every molar mass the same? Scaffold: one example could use atoms and another formula units.

Scene: [1–2](STORY.md#ch05-scene-01).

## Question 6 A fence-post repair

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q06

Use the supplied approximate neon molar mass, 20.18 g/mol. A sample contains 3.00 mol of neon atoms. A learner calculates 3.00 mol × (1 mol/20.18 g). Explain what the remaining units reveal, repair the conversion, calculate the approximate mass, and make a rough magnitude check. Would canceling mol units alone guarantee success if the learner had instead used the O₂ molar mass?

Hints: Orient: the requested result is grams. Focus: the conversion factor needs mol in its denominator. Scaffold: three moles at roughly twenty grams each should be roughly sixty grams.

Scene: [6](STORY.md#ch05-scene-06).

## Question 7 From mass to two counts

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q07

A pure O₂ model sample has mass 16.0 g. Use M(O₂) ≈ 32.00 g/mol. Find its approximate amount of O₂ molecules, number of O₂ molecules, amount of oxygen atoms within those molecules, and number of constituent oxygen atoms. Explain why an exact N_A does not make all four inferred results exact for the supplied mass.

Hints: Orient: pass through amount before converting mass to number. Focus: use grams × mol/grams, then N_A. Scaffold: the molecular amount is half a mole at the supplied precision; the constituent-atom amount is twice that.

Scene: [7](STORY.md#ch05-scene-07).

## Question 8 Counting an ionic composition

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q08

A supplied pure, idealized solid is CaCl₂. A sample represents 0.200 mol of CaCl₂ formula units. Find the amounts of Ca²⁺ ions and Cl⁻ ions, and their total ion amount. Explain why “0.200 mol of CaCl₂ molecules” is inappropriate for this ordinary ionic-solid model, and why the charge 2+ is not an extra factor in the ion count.

Hints: Orient: use one Ca²⁺ to two Cl⁻ per formula-unit composition. Focus: the subscript and charge have different jobs. Scaffold: the chloride-ion amount is twice the formula-unit amount.

Scene: [17](STORY.md#ch05-scene-17).

## Question 9 Removing is not replacing

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q09

An artificial collection contains eighty 10 u model atoms and twenty 11 u model atoms. In case A, remove ten heavy atoms without adding anything. In case B, replace ten heavy atoms with ten light atoms. Find the mean mass in each resulting collection. Explain why the two means differ even though both have ten fewer heavy atoms than the original.

Hints: Orient: count all remaining atoms in each case. Focus: case A has ninety atoms; case B has one hundred. Scaffold: calculate total assigned mass divided by the current number of atoms.

Scene: [11](STORY.md#ch05-scene-11).

## Question 10 Two batches behind two cards

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q10

Artificial 10 u/11 u model: Batch A has ten atoms, all assigned 10 u. Batch B has thirty atoms, with fifteen assigned 10 u and fifteen assigned 11 u. Find each batch mean and the mean after combining the batches. Why is the unweighted average of the two batch means not the requested pooled mean? What additional information would be needed if only the two batch means were supplied?

Hints: Orient: the pooled population consists of atoms, not summary cards. Focus: Batch B contributes three times as many atoms. Scaffold: the pooled collection has twenty-five light and fifteen heavy atoms.

Scene: [13](STORY.md#ch05-scene-13).

## Question 11 Fifty percent of which total?

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q11

Using the artificial 10 u/11 u isotope model, a collection contains 110 u of assigned mass from each kind. Find the number of model atoms of each kind, the heavy atom-number fraction, and the average mass per atom. Explain why using 0.50 as each atom-number fraction would be wrong even though each kind contributes 50% of the mass.

Hints: Orient: equal masses do not imply equal counts. Focus: divide each mass contribution by its assigned mass per atom. Scaffold: one pan has eleven light model atoms and the other ten heavy model atoms.

Scene: [14](STORY.md#ch05-scene-14).

## Question 12 A mean outside the model

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q12

A receipt claims a collection contains only the artificial 24 u and 26 u model isotopes but has average atomic mass 26.4 u. Explain why this is incompatible with the stated model. Show what the reverse-mixture calculation produces and interpret the result. Name two things you could check next without assuming which one is wrong.

Hints: Orient: a weighted mean with nonnegative fractions lies between the possible values. Focus: set f = (26.4 − 24)/(26 − 24). Scaffold: a fraction above one does not describe a permitted two-isotope mixture.

Scene: [12](STORY.md#ch05-scene-12).

## Question 13 A sample and its population

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q13

A large artificial model population is stipulated to contain 80% 10 u atoms and 20% 11 u atoms. A randomly selected ten-atom sample contains nine light and one heavy atom. Calculate the sample mean and the population mean. Does their difference prove that the population label is wrong? Would selecting a much larger sample containing only deliberately chosen heavy atoms automatically solve the representativeness problem?

Hints: Orient: identify the boundary of each mean. Focus: a small sample need not exactly reproduce population proportions. Scaffold: the sample mean describes those ten; deliberately selecting one type changes the sampling process.

Scene: [15](STORY.md#ch05-scene-15).

## Question 14 Four labels for careful reporting

ID: luminara.adventure.005.q14

A learner writes: “The Avogadro constant is about 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ by definition. Because it is exact, a weighed sample's inferred amount is exact. Atomic weight has units of u, and every decimal atomic mass proves a fractional neutron.” Repair these four statements. Also distinguish average atomic mass in u from molar mass in g/mol. You may consult the glossary and sources; no memorized digits are required.

Hints: Orient: separate an exact definition, a rounded working value, measurements, and different quantities. Focus: relative atomic mass/atomic weight is dimensionless. Scaffold: an individual real isotope mass can be noninteger in u while the nucleus contains integer numbers of protons and neutrons.

Scenes: [2](STORY.md#ch05-scene-02), [7](STORY.md#ch05-scene-07), [9](STORY.md#ch05-scene-09), [16](STORY.md#ch05-scene-16).
