## luminara.adventure.005.card01

**Definition.** What quantity does the unit mole measure when the named entities are O₂ molecules?

**Answer:** Amount of substance of those specified O₂ molecules. One mole corresponds to exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ such molecules.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Include the entity and the counting meaning. A mole is not a mass unit.

**Story:** [A number with room beneath it](STORY.md#ch05-scene-02)

## luminara.adventure.005.card02

**Definition.** What is the Avogadro constant used to connect?

**Answer:** Amount of substance n in mol with the number N of specified entities: N = nN_A, where N_A is exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

**Reasoning to recognize:** An equivalent verbal conversion is enough. Keep the per-mole unit and the entity label.

**Story:** [A number with room beneath it](STORY.md#ch05-scene-02)

## luminara.adventure.005.card03

**Definition.** What does the supplied molar mass 32.00 g/mol mean for O₂?

**Answer:** Approximately 32.00 grams per mole of O₂ molecules at the precision of the supplied value.

**Reasoning to recognize:** State mass per mole of the specified substance. The rounded molar mass is not made exact by the exact Avogadro constant.

## luminara.adventure.005.card04

**Definition.** What makes two atoms isotopes of the same element?

**Answer:** They have the same proton number and different neutron numbers.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Both features matter. Different electron counts alone identify ionization differences, not different isotopes.

## luminara.adventure.005.card05

**Contrast.** How does one mole of O₂ molecules differ from one mole of oxygen atoms within those molecules?

**Answer:** One mole of O₂ molecules contains two moles of oxygen atoms within them. The entity label determines which count is being reported.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Use the two-atoms-per-molecule relationship. The atoms need not be separate free atoms.

## luminara.adventure.005.card06

**Contrast.** Why can equal amounts of Ne atoms and O₂ molecules have different masses?

**Answer:** Equal mole amounts give equal numbers of the named entities, but Ne atoms and O₂ molecules have different masses, so their molar masses differ.

**Reasoning to recognize:** A count-versus-mass explanation is sufficient. Equal amount does not mean equal mass or equal total atom count.

## luminara.adventure.005.card07

**Contrast.** In the artificial 24 u/26 u isotope model, how does a sample’s average mass differ from an individual atom’s mass?

**Answer:** The average summarizes the atom-number-weighted mixture. Each modeled atom still has its assigned 24 u or 26 u mass, not necessarily the average.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Reject fractional-neutron interpretations of the mean. These invented masses do not assert that real isotope masses are integers.

## luminara.adventure.005.card08

**Contrast.** How do the quantities labeled u, dimensionless relative atomic mass, and g/mol differ?

**Answer:** u can label an atomic or average atomic mass; relative atomic mass is a dimensionless ratio to the atomic mass constant; g/mol labels molar mass.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Credit the distinction of quantities and units. Their familiar numerical correspondence does not authorize swapping unit labels or claiming an exact post-2019 SI identity.

**Story:** [Three quantities with similar numbers](STORY.md#ch05-scene-16)
**Support:** [Atomic mass, relative atomic mass, and molar mass](SUPPORT.md#relative-atomic-mass-atomic-weight)

**Application.** In a thought experiment containing exactly 0.25 mol of N₂O₄ molecules, how many molecules are represented?

**Answer:** 1.50553519 × 10²³ N₂O₄ molecules, from 0.25 mol × 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Keep the molecule entity and show the amount-to-count conversion. Exactness here follows the explicitly exact thought-experiment amount and constant.

## luminara.adventure.005.card10

**Application.** Exactly 0.25 mol of N₂O₄ molecules contains how many moles of oxygen atoms within those molecules?

**Answer:** Exactly 1 mol of oxygen atoms, because each N₂O₄ molecule contains four O atoms and 0.25 × 4 = 1.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Use four O atoms per molecule, not six total atoms per molecule. Those oxygen atoms remain within the molecules.

## luminara.adventure.005.card11

**Application.** Using the supplied approximate M(O₂) = 32.00 g/mol, what mass corresponds to 2.00 mol of O₂ molecules?

**Answer:** 64.0 g at three significant figures: 2.00 mol × 32.00 g/mol = 64.00 g before rounding.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Credit dimensional cancellation to grams and appropriate precision. More digits in a calculator result do not make the inputs exact.

## luminara.adventure.005.card12

**Application.** For an explicitly artificial mixture with 75% of atoms assigned 24 u and 25% assigned 26 u, what is the average atomic mass?

**Answer:** 24.5 u, from 0.75(24 u) + 0.25(26 u).

**Reasoning to recognize:** Weight by fractions of atom number, not by mass fractions or an unweighted midpoint. The answer lies closer to 24 u because those model atoms are more abundant.

**Story:** [Eighty, twenty, and the missing middle](STORY.md#ch05-scene-10)
