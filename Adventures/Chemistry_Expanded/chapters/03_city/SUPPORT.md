Story ID: luminara.adventure.003

Read the [story](STORY.md) continuously or use these supports at any point. The [practice](PRACTICE.md) and [reasoned key](TEACHER_KEY.md) are optional. No speed, memorized ion list, rotating model, or personal disclosure is required.

## The four distinctions that organize this chapter

1. **Ion formation versus ionic interaction.** Electron-loss/gain bookkeeping can explain ion charge. It is not the attraction holding a solid together or a complete account of a reaction's energy. See [scene 6](STORY.md#ch03-scene-06).
2. **Local neighborhood versus bulk composition.** Six nearest opposite-charge neighbors in ordinary NaCl do not imply a six-to-one formula. See [scenes 4–5](STORY.md#ch03-scene-04).
3. **Internal bonds versus whole-species identity.** NH₄⁺ has internal covalent bonding and is a polyatomic ion. See [scenes 10–11](STORY.md#ch03-scene-10).
4. **Ion ratio versus molecular atom counts.** Simplify counts of intact ions when writing the conventional ionic formula. Preserve the stated actual atoms per molecule in a molecular formula. See [scenes 14–16](STORY.md#ch03-scene-14).

## Contextual glossary

### Ion, cation and anion

An **ion** is a species with net electric charge. A **cation** is positive; an **anion** is negative. For simple monatomic bookkeeping, net charge in units of the elementary charge is proton count minus electron count. Ordinary electron gain or loss does not change the nucleus or element identity. Na⁺ has eleven protons and ten electrons; Cl⁻ has seventeen protons and eighteen electrons. A species can be an ion without being monatomic. Charge conservation preserves a closed system’s total charge; it does not require that total to be zero. [Scene 6](STORY.md#ch03-scene-06); sources CH03-S01, CH03-S04, CH03-S13, CH03-S17.

### Monatomic versus polyatomic ion

A **monatomic ion** consists of one atom with a net charge, such as Ca²⁺. A **polyatomic ion** contains more than one bonded atom and has a net charge for the whole group, such as NH₄⁺ or NO₃⁻. The superscript applies to the whole written ion. It does not belong only to the final element symbol. [Scenes 10–13](STORY.md#ch03-scene-10); CH03-S01, CH03-S05.

### Molecule

In this chapter a molecule is a discrete neutral bonded entity, consistent with the cited IUPAC definition. N₂ and CO₂ are examples. A molecule can contain only one element; two atoms do not automatically make a compound. Charged multiatom entities are called polyatomic ions here. Other contexts sometimes use “molecular ion”; that wording does not erase the charge distinction. [Scene 10](STORY.md#ch03-scene-10); CH03-S02, CH03-S05.

### Elemental substance, compound and molecular compound

An **elemental substance** contains one element; elemental molecules such as N₂ and O₂ are possible. A **compound** is a substance containing more than one element chemically combined. A **molecular compound** consists of molecules containing different elements, such as CO₂. A single CO₂ or H₂O entity is a molecule; “molecular compound” classifies the corresponding substance. “Covalent” describes bonding and cannot alone decide whether a substance is elemental or a compound. [Scene 10](STORY.md#ch03-scene-10); CH03-S05, CH03-S06.

### Ionic compound

An ionic compound has cations and anions in an overall neutral composition. The introductory metal/nonmetal shortcut is useful for many familiar examples but is not a universal classification proof. Ammonium salts supply a clear no-metal counterexample. Formula building requires supplied or independently justified ion identities. Neutrality does not by itself establish synthesis, stability, structure, solubility or existence of an imagined substance. [Scenes 12, 14 and 16](STORY.md#ch03-scene-12); CH03-S04, CH03-S05.

### Ionic and covalent bonding

The ionic description emphasizes electrostatic interactions among charged species. Covalent bonding concerns shared electron density in the bond. These are useful models, not a claim that every real bond lies at a perfectly pure endpoint. Electron transfer is not itself the force holding the later lattice together; neither shared density nor transfer should be interpreted as electrons following visible paths like thrown balls. [Scenes 6–7](STORY.md#ch03-scene-06), [scene 10](STORY.md#ch03-scene-10); CH03-S04, CH03-S07, CH03-S16.

### Lattice, crystal structure and local coordination

The narrative uses **lattice** in the introductory sense of an extended repeating structural arrangement. More precisely, crystallography distinguishes a mathematical lattice of repeating points from the basis associated with those points. A **local coordination number** counts nearest neighbors under the relevant structural definition. In the ordinary rock-salt structure, each interior Na⁺ has six nearest Cl⁻ neighbors and each interior Cl⁻ has six nearest Na⁺ neighbors. This is not asserted for every ionic solid, surface, pressure condition or phase. The ordinary-solid boundary also matters because isolated NaCl molecules are known in other settings. [Scenes 2, 4 and 5](STORY.md#ch03-scene-02), [scene 17](STORY.md#ch03-scene-17); CH03-S03, CH03-S04, CH03-S18.

### Bulk ratio, finite window and formula unit

A **bulk composition ratio** describes the material's composition, while a selected **finite window** may include incomplete neighborhoods or emphasize boundaries. A **formula unit** specifies the represented composition of a nonmolecular substance such as an ionic solid. For ordinary NaCl it expresses a 1:1 sodium-to-chloride ratio. It does not establish independent NaCl molecules or uniquely paired ions in the crystal. Counting a formula unit is meaningful without assigning every ion a permanent partner. [Scene 5](STORY.md#ch03-scene-05); CH03-S03, CH03-S04, CH03-S06.

### Molecular versus empirical formula

A **molecular formula** gives the actual atom counts per molecule. An **empirical formula** gives the simplest whole-number element ratio. N₂O₄ has empirical formula NO₂, but its molecular formula must remain N₂O₄. The empirical ratio alone does not uniquely recover the molecular formula. Ionic formula simplification must preserve complete polyatomic-ion identities; it is not an instruction to divide every visible atom subscript. [Scenes 15–16](STORY.md#ch03-scene-15); CH03-S06, CH03-S12.

### Valence-electron budget and Lewis drawing

For the limited NH₄⁺ example, neutral nitrogen contributes five valence electrons and each neutral hydrogen contributes one. Adjust the total once for the whole charge: 5 + 4(1) − 1 = 8. Four bond lines represent four shared pairs, using eight electrons. The drawing has no nitrogen lone pair. A whole-species electron inventory counts each pair once; each atom's local shell check can include the same bonding pair. This bridge is not a complete Lewis-structure course. [Scene 11](STORY.md#ch03-scene-11); CH03-S08, CH03-S09.

### Connectivity, geometry and formal charge

A flat drawing can show which atoms connect without reproducing their spatial arrangement. NH₄⁺ has four bonding regions and no central lone pair in the introductory model, giving tetrahedral geometry. A superscript net charge is not a physical dot floating beside an atom. Formal charges in Lewis structures are bookkeeping assignments; they must not be confused with a complete spatial charge distribution. No general geometry algorithm or formal-charge exercise is required in this chapter. [Scene 11](STORY.md#ch03-scene-11); CH03-S08, CH03-S09, CH03-S10.

## Every symbol has one job in its context

- **Na versus Na⁺ in the arrival ledger:** a neutral sodium atom versus a sodium ion with charge +1. Elsewhere Na can label the element or its contribution within a formula; the context still matters.
- **O₂ versus O²⁻ versus O₂²⁻:** two oxygen atoms in the stated molecular species; one oxide ion with charge −2; one peroxide ion with two oxygen atoms and total charge −2. The identity must be supplied or checked.
- **NH₄⁺:** four hydrogens in the ion, net charge +1 for the entire group.
- **(NH₄)₂CO₃:** two whole ammonium groups and one carbonate group; N 2, H 8, C 1, O 3.
- **Ca(NO₃)₂:** one calcium and two nitrate groups; Ca 1, N 2, O 6.
- **2 Ca(NO₃)₂:** two formula units in the specified inventory; Ca 2, nitrate groups 4, N 4, O 12. The coefficient multiplies the whole formula-unit count. It does not change the identity of the compound.
- **2 NH₄⁺:** two ammonium ions; each has charge +1, so their combined net charge is +2. This expression does not describe a newly bonded molecule.

Unambiguous ASCII may be used: Ca(NO3)2, NH4^+, O2^2-. If a typed expression is ambiguous, ask what the writer intended before diagnosing a chemistry error. Symbol case matters: Co and CO are different.

## An optional whole-ion formula ladder

1. Identify the actual supplied ions and their signed charges.
2. Treat each polyatomic ion as intact for this task.
3. Choose positive whole-number ion counts whose charges sum to zero.
4. Reduce a common factor in the **whole-ion counts**, if present.
5. Put parentheses around a polyatomic group when an outside subscript repeats it.
6. Check the atom inventory separately from charge and read the notation aloud.
7. State what the result does not establish: the arithmetic is not an actual preparation, full structure determination or safety assessment.

Worked contrast: four Na⁺ and two O₂²⁻ have charges +4 and −4. Reduce their whole-ion ratio 4:2 to 2:1. The result remains Na₂O₂ because the two oxygens belong to each intact peroxide ion. By contrast, supplied Ca²⁺ and O²⁻ need one of each, giving CaO. [Scene 16](STORY.md#ch03-scene-16).

## Optional deeper support: why attraction does not mean collapse

The force/energy excursion distinguishes charge bookkeeping from present interactions. Attraction between opposite charges is important, while short-range effects associated with electronic structure oppose unlimited compression. A schematic total-potential-energy minimum illustrates a stable equilibrium spacing. It is not a numerical NaCl energy calculation or a claim that a crystal is reducible to isolated pairs. A many-particle account includes the whole arrangement; nearest-neighbor lines are a readable structural subset, not all interactions. Real solids also have vibrational motion and imperfections. [Scene 7](STORY.md#ch03-scene-07); CH03-S07, CH03-S14, CH03-S15.

## Text-only descriptions of the optional images

- **Ordinary NaCl local view:** choose an interior Na⁺ position. Its six nearest Cl⁻ neighbors lie on the positive and negative directions of three perpendicular axes. A selected Cl⁻ has six nearest Na⁺ neighbors. These overlapping neighborhoods do not change the overall 1:1 composition.
- **Counting analogy:** an even-by-even, two-dimensional alternating plus/minus grid has equal totals although an interior position has four nearest opposite signs. It illustrates overlapping local counts; it is not the three-dimensional NaCl structure.
- **Ammonium:** one N bonded to four H atoms in a tetrahedral arrangement, net charge +1 for the whole ion. No color or rotation is needed to recover this information.
- **Energy sketch:** a qualitative attraction contribution lowers energy with decreasing separation, a steep short-range repulsive contribution rises at small separation, and their schematic total has a minimum. No numerical values or experimental fit are implied.
