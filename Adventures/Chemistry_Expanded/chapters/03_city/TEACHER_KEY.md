# Teacher guide and reasoned key

Story ID: luminara.adventure.003

This key supports optional self-study and discussion. It is not an instructor-approved assessment, production validator or claim of automated semantic grading. The [practice](PRACTICE.md) keeps the original four question texts and adds ten original transfer items. Supply the ion identities and charges stated in each prompt; recall of an ion list is not the target.

## Teaching purpose and response options

Separate species identity, bond description, structural scope and formula counting. A learner may explain in prose, speak, use an annotated diagram, or give equations with a short explanation. An accurate ordinary-language explanation is sufficient; the story's exact phrasing is not required. Color, motion, memorization, timed performance and lengthy prose are not prerequisites.

Check the present answer. Acknowledge exactly what a revision repaired instead of repeating an old correction. A sound formula with an uncertain explanation and an incorrect formula with a good partial model need different feedback. The first useful question is often “What is your whole counted unit?”

## luminara.adventure.003.q01

- O₂ is an **elemental molecule**: neutral, discrete, and containing only oxygen.
- H₂O represents a neutral discrete **molecule of the molecular compound water**, containing different elements. “Molecular compound” classifies the substance; “molecule” identifies the individual entity.
- NH₄⁺ is a **polyatomic ion**: a charged bonded group with more than one atom.

All three contain covalent bonds in the supplied introductory account. That bond description alone does not settle charge, number of elements or whole-species category. If O₂ is called a compound, preserve the recognition that it has two atoms and repair the distinction between number of atoms and kinds of elements. If NH₄⁺ is classified only as a covalent compound, ask what the whole-species superscript means.

## luminara.adventure.003.q02

One Ca²⁺ requires two whole NO₃⁻ ions: +2 + 2(−1) = 0. The ion ratio is calcium:nitrate = 1:2, and the formula is **Ca(NO₃)₂**. Atom inventory: Ca 1, N 2, O 6. The outside two repeats both N and O₃ as a whole nitrate group; nitrate's internal N:O ratio remains 1:3.

If CaNO₃ is proposed, the indicated ion charges total +1. If CaN₂O₃ is proposed, the second nitrate's three oxygens have been lost. If the learner supplies the correct expanded atom inventory CaN₂O₆, acknowledge the counts and coach conventional grouping as Ca(NO₃)₂. Atom totals and preferred ionic notation are separate checks.

Scene: 13. Sources: CH03-S05, CH03-S06; arithmetic authored from supplied charges.

## luminara.adventure.003.q03

The least common charge magnitude is 6. Two Al³⁺ ions supply +6; three O²⁻ ions supply −6. **Al₂O₃** is neutral in the simplest whole-ion ratio. A doubled set, Al₄O₆, preserves neutrality but not the requested simplest conventional ratio.

A molecular formula reports actual atom counts per molecule. N₂O₄ must remain **N₂O₄** when its molecular formula is requested. NO₂ is its empirical formula and, as a molecular formula, describes different per-molecule atom counts. Equal elemental ratios do not make molecular formulas interchangeable.

Scenes: 14–15. Sources: CH03-S04, CH03-S06.

## luminara.adventure.003.q04

Six describes the nearest-neighbor environment of one selected interior sodium ion in the ordinary rock-salt structure. The neighbors are not six chlorides uniquely owned by that sodium. Neighborhoods overlap; each interior chloride likewise has six nearest sodium neighbors. The bulk sodium-to-chloride composition remains 1:1, so the formula is NaCl, not NaCl₆.

No fractional unit-cell calculation is required. The explanation should not say the six chlorides split into fractional physical ions or that only nearest neighbors interact. Distinguish a local view, an arbitrarily cropped fragment and the bulk ratio.

Scenes: 4–5. Sources: CH03-S03, CH03-S04.

## luminara.adventure.003.q05

Sodium: 11 protons and 10 electrons after losing one electron, so charge is 11 − 10 = +1. Chlorine: 17 protons and 18 electrons after gaining one electron, so charge is 17 − 18 = −1. The neutral-atom electron counts were 11 and 17. The combined total charge stays zero in this stated example and the nuclei retain their element identities. Conservation means an unchanged total for a system exchanging no charge with its surroundings, not a requirement that every chosen system be neutral.

The ribbon adds an unsupported exclusive-partner claim. Electron bookkeeping does not establish one permanent Na⁺–Cl⁻ molecule in the ordinary solid, describe all present interactions, or give a complete energetic/reaction pathway. Removing an electron from an isolated atom has an energy cost; a real overall process needs the relevant complete account.

Scenes: 3, 6–7. Sources: CH03-S04, CH03-S07, CH03-S13, CH03-S17.

## luminara.adventure.003.q06

The four negative neighbors in one local view are not assigned exclusively to the selected positive sign. They recur in other local views. Counting all positions on the supplied even-by-even alternating board gives equal positive and negative totals, while counting the selected neighborhood answers a different question.

The board is a two-dimensional analogy. Its fourfold local pattern is not the sixfold three-dimensional environment of ordinary NaCl, and no analogy alone establishes an actual crystal structure. The actual structural claim comes from the stated ordinary rock-salt model and its scientific sources.

Scenes: 4–5. Sources: CH03-S03, CH03-S04. The checkerboard argument is an authored mathematical analogy.

## luminara.adventure.003.q07

Two NH₄⁺ and one CO₃²⁻ give 2(+1) + (−2) = 0, yielding **(NH₄)₂CO₃**. The represented atoms are N 2, H 8, C 1 and O 3. Parentheses preserve the complete ammonium group when repeating it.

Each ammonium ion and carbonate ion is a meaningful whole polyatomic species in this task. Selecting two ammoniums and one carbonate for composition bookkeeping does not establish a separate three-ion molecule in the ionic solid or assign each cation to one exclusive anion. Internal covalent bonding in the polyatomic ions coexists with ionic classification of the neutral salt.

Scene: 12. Sources: CH03-S04, CH03-S05; charge and atom arithmetic authored.

## luminara.adventure.003.q08

**3 Ca(NO₃)₂** represents:

- 3 formula units
- 3 calcium ions
- 6 nitrate ions
- 6 nitrogen atoms
- 18 oxygen atoms

The leading 3 multiplies the entire formula-unit inventory. The outside 2 counts nitrate groups per formula unit. The internal 3 counts oxygen atoms in each nitrate. The equal-looking digits have different scopes. Charges of the supplied ions are not encoded by these subscripts or the coefficient. The three-unit inventory is neutral because it contains 3(+2) + 6(−1) = 0.

Scenes: 13, 17. Sources: CH03-S05, CH03-S06; arithmetic authored.

## luminara.adventure.003.q09

The tiles contain two complete nitrates, hence two N and six O. **CaN₂O₃** retains both nitrogens but only one nitrate's three oxygens. The conventional repaired formula is **Ca(NO₃)₂**. An expanded inventory may state Ca 1, N 2, O 6.

A zero charge reading based on the original tiles verifies their selected charge total. It does not verify that a separate printer preserved the ion identities or atom counts. The two records must be compared. Do not diagnose the original tile selection as nonneutral when the failure is in the later transcription.

## luminara.adventure.003.q10

Mg₂(SO₄)₂ has total charge 2(+2) + 2(−2) = 0 under the supplied whole-ion interpretation. Its whole-ion counts 2:2 reduce to 1:1, so the conventional formula is **MgSO₄**. Divide the counts of Mg²⁺ ions and SO₄²⁻ groups by two. Do not divide sulfate's internal oxygen subscript four; that would change the supplied ion identity.

One sulfate group requires no outside subscript or parentheses in the usual formula here. This arithmetic does not independently predict all properties or a preparation method.

Scenes: 12, 14, 16. Sources: CH03-S04, CH03-S05; transfer arithmetic authored.

## luminara.adventure.003.q11

For supplied Li⁺ and H⁻, +1 + (−1) = 0 gives **LiH**. Replacing H⁻ with H⁺ would contradict the stated hydride identity and would make one of each positively charged overall.

For supplied Na⁺ and O₂²⁻, 2(+1) + (−2) = 0 gives **Na₂O₂**. The whole-ion ratio is 2 sodium ions to 1 peroxide ion, already simplest. The oxygen subscript two belongs inside the peroxide ion. Reducing visible atom subscripts to NaO would destroy that identity. A doubled accounting set of four sodium ions and two peroxide ions reduces to the same Na₂O₂ formula.

Do not infer safe handling, reactivity or synthesis conditions from these formulas. The examples test supplied identity rather than a universal charge prediction.

Scene: 16. Sources: CH03-S11, CH03-S12; arithmetic authored.

## luminara.adventure.003.q12

The information establishes the simplest nitrogen-to-oxygen ratio and hence **NO₂ as an empirical formula**, not a unique molecular formula. Both NO₂ and N₂O₄ have a 1:2 elemental ratio. Independently determined atom counts per molecule, or a molecular mass combined with the empirical composition, could identify the molecular multiplier.

Do not require a numerical mass calculation; no mass data are supplied. The learner need not claim that every imaginable integer multiple corresponds to a stable molecule. The point is underdetermination from the ratio alone.

Scene: 15. Source: CH03-S06; inference from information content authored.

## luminara.adventure.003.q13

Budget: 5 + 4(1) − 1 = **8 valence electrons**. Positive charge subtracts one from the sum of neutral-atom contributions. Four N–H single bonds use four shared pairs, **8 electrons**, leaving **no lone pair on nitrogen** in this Lewis structure. Each hydrogen has its duet and nitrogen has an octet in local checks; those checks do not increase the total inventory.

Four bonding regions and no central lone pair give tetrahedral geometry under the supplied introductory model. A flat cross can record connectivity while projecting or rearranging spatial directions for the page. It is not evidence of a planar ion. No exact angle, hybridization explanation, formal-charge calculation or pronunciation is required.

The superscript + reports the whole ion's net charge. Do not describe it as one positive particle located beside the fourth hydrogen.

Scene: 11. Sources: CH03-S08, CH03-S09, CH03-S10.

## luminara.adventure.003.q14

One acceptable repair:

> Ordinary solid sodium chloride is an extended ionic structure with a 1:1 ratio of sodium ions to chloride ions, not a collection of permanently assigned NaCl molecular couples. Electron-transfer bookkeeping helps describe ion formation, while present electrostatic interactions and the full electronic structure are relevant to the solid's behavior. A nearest-neighbor picture is a limited structural view; it does not include every interaction or follow from charge balance alone.

Other sound answers may mention six opposite-charge nearest neighbors in the ordinary interior, the distinction between charge balance and structure, or the limitations of the schematic energy model. The replacement must not claim ions are immobile forever, that short-range repulsion is absent, or that all ionic compounds have sixfold coordination.

## Feedback and model limits

- Preserve partial success: right ion identities, right charge sum, right group count and readable notation are separable achievements.
- If a learner “crosses charges,” ask them to verify the resulting whole-ion ratio and neutrality. Do not require one procedural trick when another sound method works.
- A polyatomic ion is not a forever-sealed object. Its internal identity is held fixed for this specified formula task, while actual chemistry can change species.
- A balance reading is charge arithmetic, not mass. The fiction includes no actual chemical handling, removal of real crystal ions or laboratory observations.
- The flat checkerboard, light controls, carrier and ribbons are teaching constructions. They demonstrate representational consequences, not new experimental evidence.
- The energy scene is qualitative and many-particle-scoped. It uses no measured NaCl curve, equilibrium distance or numerical lattice energy.
- The ammonium bridge is limited to this supplied example. It does not cover Unit II geometry, resonance, exceptions or instructor-specific drawing conventions comprehensively.
- The first four original question texts remain unchanged. New questions are companion practice, never replacements for source worksheet items.
