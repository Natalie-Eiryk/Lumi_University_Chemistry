## luminara.adventure.003.card01

**Definition.** What is a lattice in the chapter’s ordinary solid NaCl model?

**Answer:** An extended repeating arrangement of ions in three dimensions, rather than a collection of separate NaCl couples.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Accept a repeated ionic network with the solid-state scope. Do not require a drawing or a particular viewing angle.

## luminara.adventure.003.card02

**Definition.** What does one NaCl formula unit specify for the ordinary ionic solid?

**Answer:** One sodium ion for each chloride ion in the represented composition ratio. It does not identify an isolated two-ion molecule in the crystal.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Credit the 1:1 composition-unit meaning and the absence of special separate pairs.

**Story:** [Six Neighbors, One-to-One Composition](STORY.md#ch03-scene-05)

## luminara.adventure.003.card03

**Definition.** Why is NH₄⁺ called a polyatomic ion?

**Answer:** It is a bonded group of more than one atom with an overall nonzero charge, here +1.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Include both multiple atoms and net charge. Its internal N–H covalent bonds do not cancel the whole ion’s charge.

**Story:** [Three Displays and Two Bad Drawers](STORY.md#ch03-scene-10); [A Boundary Worth Keeping](STORY.md#ch03-scene-11)

## luminara.adventure.003.card04

**Definition.** What is an ionic compound in the chapter’s supplied simple models?

**Answer:** An electrically neutral substance composed of cations and anions, such as ordinary solid NaCl or ammonium carbonate.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Include positive and negative ions in an overall neutral composition. A metal is not part of the definition.

**Story:** [The Whole Ion Comes Along](STORY.md#ch03-scene-12)

## luminara.adventure.003.card05

**Contrast.** Why is N₂ a molecule but not a compound?

**Answer:** Its two bonded atoms form a discrete neutral molecule, but both atoms are nitrogen; a compound contains more than one element.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Distinguish number of atoms from number of elements. The presence of a covalent bond alone does not make a compound.

**Story:** [Three Displays and Two Bad Drawers](STORY.md#ch03-scene-10)
**Support:** [Molecule versus compound](SUPPORT.md#elemental-substance-compound-and-molecular-compound)

**Related practice:** [luminara.adventure.003.q01](PRACTICE.md#luminaraadventure003q01) (preserved original question)

**Method transfer:** The scene works N₂ and CO₂. Original q01 transfers the same molecule/element/compound distinction to O₂ and H₂O and also tests NH₄⁺.

## luminara.adventure.003.card06

**Contrast.** How can NH₄⁺ have covalent bonds and still be an ion?

**Answer:** Covalent describes its internal N–H bonding; ion describes the overall charged species. Those labels answer different questions.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Credit the distinction between bonding inside a group and net charge of the whole group.

**Story:** [Three Displays and Two Bad Drawers](STORY.md#ch03-scene-10)

## luminara.adventure.003.card07

**Contrast.** In ordinary solid NaCl, why does six nearest opposite-charge neighbors not mean a NaCl₆ formula?

**Answer:** Six describes a local coordination environment; NaCl records the overall 1:1 ion composition throughout the extended lattice.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Contrast local neighbor count with bulk ratio. Neighbors are not exclusively owned by the central ion.

## luminara.adventure.003.card08

**Contrast.** How does electron-transfer bookkeeping differ from the interaction holding the NaCl lattice together?

**Answer:** Transfer bookkeeping tracks electron gain and loss in an introductory formation model. Ionic attraction concerns interactions among charges in the resulting structure.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Explain why a remembered unique donor–recipient partnership is not the lattice’s binding mechanism.

## luminara.adventure.003.card09

**Application.** What ratio of intact NH₄⁺ ions to CO₃²⁻ ions makes a neutral ammonium carbonate composition?

**Answer:** 2:1. Two +1 charges balance one −2 charge.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Accept a charge sum 2(+1) + (−2) = 0 or a labeled tile explanation. Internal ion subscripts stay unchanged.

## luminara.adventure.003.card10

**Application.** In Ca(NO₃)₂, what does the outside subscript 2 multiply?

**Answer:** The entire nitrate group NO₃: it represents two nitrate ions, with two nitrogen atoms and six oxygen atoms in the formula-unit composition.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Credit whole-group multiplication; Ca is outside those parentheses and is not multiplied by this 2.

## luminara.adventure.003.card11

**Application.** Using Al³⁺ and O²⁻, what simplest whole-ion ratio makes a neutral formula?

**Answer:** Two aluminum ions to three oxide ions, giving Al₂O₃: +6 and −6 balance.

**Reasoning to recognize:** A smallest whole-number charge-balance explanation is sufficient. Neutrality alone does not prove a proposed substance’s stability or synthesis.

## luminara.adventure.003.card12

**Application.** Ammonium carbonate is known to contain NH₄⁺ and CO₃²⁻. Does its lack of a metal make it molecular?

**Answer:** No. The verified cation and anion identities identify an ionic compound; the cation here is ammonium.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Use the supplied identities rather than a metal/nonmetal shortcut. This is not a claim that every metal-free formula is ionic.
