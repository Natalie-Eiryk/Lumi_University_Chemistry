# Optional practice for The City Without Couples

Story ID: luminara.adventure.003

These are explanation and transfer tasks, not a compulsory ending. References and [support](SUPPORT.md) remain available. The first four question IDs and question texts are preserved from the original collection. New IDs q05–q14 identify ten newly authored companion questions. Hints are optional; the separate [teacher key](TEACHER_KEY.md) may be used whenever helpful.

## luminara.adventure.003.q01

Classify O₂, H₂O, and NH₄⁺ using the supplied facts: O₂ and H₂O are neutral discrete molecules; NH₄⁺ is a charged bonded group. Which is elemental, which is a molecular compound, and which is a polyatomic ion? Explain why “has covalent bonds” is not a complete classification.

## luminara.adventure.003.q02

Build neutral calcium nitrate from Ca²⁺ and NO₃⁻. State the ion ratio, formula, and total numbers of Ca, N, and O represented. Explain the parentheses.

## luminara.adventure.003.q03

Build the simplest neutral ionic formula from Al³⁺ and O²⁻. Show the charge accounting. Then explain why the instruction to reduce an ionic ratio does not authorize changing the molecular formula N₂O₄ to NO₂.

## luminara.adventure.003.q04

In ordinary solid NaCl, an illustration shows one sodium ion surrounded by six nearest chloride neighbors. A student concludes the compound must be NaCl₆. Identify the difference between a local neighborhood and an overall composition ratio. A text explanation is sufficient.

## luminara.adventure.003.q05

An animation shows a neutral sodium atom losing one electron and a neutral chlorine atom gaining it. It then draws a permanent ribbon between the two resulting ions. Sodium has 11 protons and chlorine has 17. Identify what the electron bookkeeping establishes, calculate each final electron count and charge, and explain one claim the ribbon adds without support. Do not calculate a reaction energy.

## luminara.adventure.003.q06

A two-dimensional even-by-even checkerboard of alternating positive and negative signs has equal total counts. A selected interior positive sign has four nearest negative neighbors. Explain why writing the whole board's composition as “one positive to four negatives” would be wrong. Then state why this analogy does not establish NaCl's actual three-dimensional coordination number.

## luminara.adventure.003.q07

Use intact NH₄⁺ and CO₃²⁻ ions to build the simplest neutral formula. Show the charge sum and count N, H, C and O. A learner encloses the selected two positive ions and one negative ion in a ribbon and calls the set a molecule within the ordinary ionic solid. Explain which grouping is valid for the accounting and which additional claim is not justified.

## luminara.adventure.003.q08

For the inventory written 3 Ca(NO₃)₂, state the number of formula units, calcium ions, nitrate ions, nitrogen atoms and oxygen atoms represented. Explain the distinct roles of the leading 3, the outside subscript 2 and the internal subscript 3. Use the supplied identities Ca²⁺ and NO₃⁻; no physical preparation is implied.

## luminara.adventure.003.q09

A model's tiles correctly contain one Ca²⁺ and two NO₃⁻ ions, but its printer produces CaN₂O₃. Diagnose the exact atom-count failure, repair the conventional ionic formula, and explain why a correct zero charge reading on the original tiles does not prove that the printed formula preserved those tiles.

## luminara.adventure.003.q10

Given intact Mg²⁺ and SO₄²⁻ ions, a learner proposes Mg₂(SO₄)₂. Check neutrality, then write the simplest conventional ionic formula. Explain which two counts may be divided by a common factor and which internal subscript must remain unchanged.

## luminara.adventure.003.q11

The identities are supplied: lithium hydride is represented by Li⁺ and H⁻; sodium peroxide is represented by Na⁺ and the intact peroxide ion O₂²⁻. Build each neutral formula. Explain why hydrogen must not be silently changed to H⁺ and why the conventional sodium peroxide formula must not be reduced to NaO.

## luminara.adventure.003.q12

An unknown molecular substance is reported only to have a nitrogen-to-oxygen atom ratio of 1:2. A student concludes its molecular formula must be NO₂. Explain what the information establishes and what additional kind of information would be needed to distinguish NO₂ from N₂O₄ as molecular formulas. No actual sample analysis or reaction is requested.

## luminara.adventure.003.q13

Optional Lewis bridge: use neutral-atom valence contributions N = 5 and H = 1 to calculate the NH₄⁺ valence-electron budget. Draw or describe four N–H single bonds, count the electrons used, and state whether a lone pair remains on N. Given that four bonding regions and no central lone pair produce tetrahedral geometry in this model, explain why a flat cross drawing is not evidence of a flat ion.

## luminara.adventure.003.q14

Rewrite this misleading exhibit label in two or three clear sentences: “Every ion in salt belongs to one molecule with its favorite partner. Its electron transfer makes it stay there forever. A charge-balanced formula tells us the entire structure.” Keep the scope at ordinary solid NaCl. Include one limit of the replacement model; a detailed quantum calculation is not required.

## Progressive hints, available separately from the prompts

### q01
1. Count kinds of elements separately from total atoms.
2. Check the net charge of the whole species.
3. Neutral O₂ contains one element; neutral H₂O contains two elements; NH₄⁺ is a charged multiatom group. Internal covalent bonds do not erase these differences.

### q02
1. Compare the +2 of one calcium with the −1 of one whole nitrate.
2. Two nitrate ions supply −2.
3. Repeat the entire NO₃ group twice, using parentheses; then multiply both N and O counts.

### q03
1. List small multiples of charge magnitudes 3 and 2.
2. The first shared magnitude is 6.
3. Two Al³⁺ and three O²⁻ balance. N₂O₄ was given as a molecular formula, so its actual atom counts must remain.

### q04
1. Ask whether the six neighbors belong exclusively to the selected sodium.
2. Neighbor descriptions overlap across the lattice.
3. Each interior Cl⁻ also has six nearest Na⁺ neighbors. Local coordination and overall composition are different counts.

### q05
1. Losing a negative electron moves net charge toward positive.
2. Electron counts become 10 and 18; nuclear counts remain 11 and 17.
3. The bookkeeping does not assign a favorite partner, identify all present interactions, or supply a complete reaction/energy account.

### q06
1. A position can occur in several local-neighborhood descriptions.
2. Count the entire even-by-even board separately from a selected neighborhood.
3. The board is explicitly two-dimensional. Actual NaCl structure needs structural evidence, not an analogy alone.

### q07
1. Use whole ammonium ions; do not alter NH₄ internally.
2. Two charges of +1 balance −2.
3. Parentheses repeat all of NH₄. A formula-unit count does not establish an independent three-ion molecular object.

### q08
1. First read one Ca(NO₃)₂ formula unit.
2. One unit gives one Ca and two nitrate groups, containing two N and six O.
3. Multiply all of that by the leading coefficient 3.

### q09
1. Write the contents of each nitrate tile separately.
2. Two nitrate ions contain two N and six O, not three O.
3. The charge display checked its actual inputs. It did not check whether the later printout faithfully represented them.

### q10
1. Each Mg²⁺/SO₄²⁻ pair balances.
2. The whole-ion counts are two magnesium ions and two sulfate ions.
3. Reduce those counts to one and one; leave sulfate as SO₄²⁻.

### q11
1. Use the stated charges instead of a memorized default for hydrogen or oxygen.
2. One Li⁺ balances one H⁻; two Na⁺ balance one O₂²⁻.
3. The peroxide's internal two must survive simplification of any whole-ion counts.

### q12
1. A ratio omits an overall multiplier.
2. Both 1:2 and 2:4 simplify to 1:2.
3. A determined molecular mass, together with the empirical composition, or independently established molecular atom counts could distinguish the formulas.

### q13
1. Begin with 5 + 4(1), then adjust once for the overall positive charge.
2. Subtract one electron; four single bonds use four pairs.
3. Eight electrons are used in bonds, leaving no N lone pair. Connectivity on a page does not fix the three-dimensional shape.

### q14
1. Separate a charge-formation story from present interactions.
2. Separate a local neighbor view from a bulk ratio.
3. Mention an extended lattice, 1:1 composition, no exclusive molecular couples, and at least one honest limit such as the nearest-neighbor picture omitting more distant interactions.
