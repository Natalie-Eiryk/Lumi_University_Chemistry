## luminara.adventure.001.card01

**Definition.** For a chlorine atom or ion, what does atomic number 17 count?

**Answer:** The 17 protons in its nucleus. Proton number fixes its identity as chlorine.

**Reasoning to recognize:** A proton-count explanation is sufficient; electrons or mass number do not define atomic number.

## luminara.adventure.001.card02

**Definition.** What does ground state mean for a neutral fluorine atom in this chapter?

**Answer:** Its lowest-energy electronic state. The chapter’s configuration clues assume that state rather than an excited state.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Accept lowest-energy arrangement/state in context; do not require the phrase electronic state verbatim.

**Story:** [The staircase that was not a filling rule](STORY.md#ch01-scene-08)
**Support:** [Ground state](SUPPORT.md#configuration-notation-and-its-levels)

## luminara.adventure.001.card03

**Definition.** In the chapter’s neutral, first-twenty main-group examples, what are valence electrons?

**Answer:** Electrons in the outermost occupied principal shell, under this stated main-group convention.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Accept outer-shell electrons with the stated scope. Do not export this rule unchanged to all transition-metal contexts.

## luminara.adventure.001.card04

**Definition.** What is subshell occupancy in the notation 3p⁵?

**Answer:** The number of electrons assigned to that subshell; here the 3p subshell contains five electrons.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Recognize that five is an occupancy of 3p, not five electrons in the whole atom. Equivalent spoken or plain-text notation is welcome.

## luminara.adventure.001.card05

**Contrast.** How does the n = 3 shell differ from its 3p subshell?

**Answer:** The shell groups states with principal quantum number 3. The 3p subshell is one part of that shell, distinguished by the p subshell type.

**Reasoning to recognize:** A whole-category versus subcategory explanation is enough. Neither is a literal circular electron track.

**Story:** [Rooms inside rooms and the point where rooms fail](STORY.md#ch01-scene-06)

**Contrast.** For chlorine on a periodic table, how do period and group differ?

**Reasoning to recognize:** Identify row versus column. A temporal meaning of period does not apply here.

## luminara.adventure.001.card07

**Contrast.** Why is ns² np⁵ an outer pattern rather than a complete electron configuration for F or Cl?

**Answer:** It describes the outer s and p occupancies without specifying n or listing all the inner electrons.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Accept an explanation of missing principal-shell identity and core inventory, rather than an exact definition.

## luminara.adventure.001.card08

**Contrast.** What is the difference between saying F and Cl share a group pattern and saying they behave identically?

**Answer:** Their related outer configurations support useful chemical similarities. They do not make every size, energy, or reaction property identical.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Name the restricted similarity and reject universal identity; no particular property value is required.

## luminara.adventure.001.card09

**Application.** A neutral ground-state first-twenty atom fits ns² np⁵ and is in period 3. Which element fits?

**Answer:** Chlorine: the additional period clue selects 3s² 3p⁵ rather than fluorine’s 2s² 2p⁵.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Credit elimination of F by the period clue. Chlorine without any reasoning is a starting answer, not the whole explanation.

## luminara.adventure.001.card10

**Application.** How many electrons are represented by 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵?

**Answer:** 17 electrons: 2 + 2 + 6 + 2 + 5.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Any correct sum or labeled counting explanation is sufficient. The result is an electron inventory, not automatically an atomic number for every species.

## luminara.adventure.001.card11

**Application.** A species has 17 protons and 18 electrons. Which element does it belong to?

**Answer:** Chlorine, because its 17 protons determine the element even though the electron count differs from that of neutral chlorine.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Accept Cl or chlorine with a proton-identity explanation. Identifying the negative ion is welcome but not needed for this card’s identity question.

## luminara.adventure.001.card12

**Application.** Oxygen has outer configuration 2s² 2p⁴ and sulfur has 3s² 3p⁴. What one missing clue would distinguish them in a neutral ground-state first-twenty search?

**Answer:** The period, or equivalently the value of the outer principal quantum number n: 2 selects oxygen and 3 selects sulfur.

**Reasoning to recognize:** Any independently supplied discriminator, such as atomic number 8 versus 16, is also valid. Do not reward a guess from the shared outer pattern alone.

**Story:** [The next letter refuses to be chlorine](STORY.md#ch01-scene-17)
