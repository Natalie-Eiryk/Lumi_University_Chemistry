### H01 Moseley's primary papers

H. G. J. Moseley, “The High-Frequency Spectra of the Elements,” Philosophical Magazine, series 6, volume 26 (1913), pp. 1024–1034, [DOI 10.1080/14786441308635052](https://doi.org/10.1080/14786441308635052).

Intact primary-text passages were read through the [ChemTeam transcription of the 1913 paper and 1914 continuation](https://www.chemteam.info/Chem-History/Moseley-article.html) and compared with the [displayed excerpts at LEIFIphysik](https://www.leifiphysik.de/atomphysik/roentgen-strahlung/versuche/originalarbeit-von-moseley). These hosted versions share transcription defects and are not treated as independently verified editions. The [publisher record](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14786441308635052) was available through its search-index metadata, verifying volume, pages, and DOI; direct access was blocked. Its 2009 online date is not the original publication date. The Oxford History of Science Museum’s [catalogue of the preserved offprints](https://www.mhs.ox.ac.uk/collections/library/catalogue-simple/authors/index1e34-2.html?id=1397546) directly confirms December 1913 for Part I and April 1914 for Part II, volume 27, pp. 703–713.

The 1913 text supports photographing characteristic spectra, crystal-based wavelength analysis, attention to impurities and measurement limitations, the systematic relation of characteristic frequencies to elemental order, and an interpretation still being refined. The hosted pages also contain 1914 material; they must not be treated as one undated 1913 paper. No 1914 full-survey data, named future discoveries, or historical numerical values enter the October visitor's unbriefed account. The bus explicitly introduces its retrospective first-paper schematic before he examines it. The intact 1913 prose describes square-root-frequency regularity and acknowledges theoretical difficulties; the missing material prevents an exhaustive reconstruction of the original derivation.

**Access limitation:** the transcriptions have missing/garbled equations, omitted text, and other defects. No exact equation, raw numerical fit, instrumental uncertainty, or exhaustive sample list is reconstructed from them. A full first-paper facsimile was not independently inspected: the publisher was blocked, the MIT Part I link required institutional sign-in, and a public repository PDF could not be decoded. The repository’s inconsistent date metadata is not used. The source record supports the bounded qualitative account, not a reproduction of the original experiment.
