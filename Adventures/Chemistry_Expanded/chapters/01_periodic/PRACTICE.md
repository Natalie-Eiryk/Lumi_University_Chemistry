## luminara.adventure.001.q01

Among neutral ground-state atoms in the first twenty elements, the outer pattern ns² np⁴ fits oxygen (2s² 2p⁴) and sulfur (3s² 3p⁴). A delivery system accepts only one answer. Is the clue sufficient? What extra observation would let it choose? Explain rather than guessing.

Progressive hints, optional:
1. Which candidates does the question itself supply?
2. Compare the principal numbers in their outer patterns.
3. A period observation can retain one candidate and exclude the other.

## luminara.adventure.001.q02

A species has seventeen protons and eighteen electrons. Someone identifies it as argon because its electron count is eighteen. Use the supplied atomic numbers, Cl = 17 and Ar = 18, to repair the claim and state its charge. Does the story's original neutral-atom clue apply?

Progressive hints, optional:
1. Which particle count determines elemental identity?
2. Use proton count minus electron count for net charge in elementary-charge units.
3. Compare 17 − 18 with zero, then check the original neutrality condition.

## luminara.adventure.001.q03

A learner says the 5 in 3p⁵ means five electrons in the entire atom. Explain the scope of the 5. Then count all electrons in 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵. What additional condition allows that electron count to identify the atomic number?

Progressive hints, optional:
1. Keep each superscript attached to its own subshell label.
2. Add the superscripts, not the leading principal numbers.
3. A neutral atom has equal electron and proton counts.

## luminara.adventure.001.q05

A display contains 3p⁵, Cl⁻, “group 17,” and “atomic number 17.” Explain the job of the 3, p, and ⁵ in 3p⁵; the minus in Cl⁻; and the two different seventeens. Would matching the printed number 17 be a valid rule for assigning an atomic number to every group-17 element?

Progressive hints, optional:
1. The same typographic position need not give symbols the same role.
2. A group label describes a column; atomic number describes a nuclear count.
3. Use F: group 17, atomic number 9 as a counterexample.

## luminara.adventure.001.q06

Use these supplied neutral ground-state configurations:

- F: 1s² 2s² 2p⁵
- Cl: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁵

Give each complete electron total and each outermost-principal-level total. Write a shell-total summary for each. Explain why chlorine's five written subshell terms do not mean five occupied principal shells.

Progressive hints, optional:
1. Group terms by their leading principal number.
2. Chlorine's n = 2 shell includes both 2s and 2p.
3. Count all superscripts for the complete total; use only the highest occupied n for the outer-level total in these main-group examples.

## luminara.adventure.001.q07

A learner says, “If chlorine has no 3d term in its ground-state configuration, there cannot be a 3d subshell. Also, every shell can hold only eight electrons.” Using the supplied capacities s = 2, p = 6, and d = 10, distinguish an allowed subshell from its occupancy and compare the possible capacities of shells n = 2 and n = 3. Do not infer a universal filling order from the capacities.

Progressive hints, optional:
1. The n = 2 shell has s and p; n = 3 has s, p, and d.
2. Zero occupancy is different from an unavailable category.
3. Add 2 + 6 and 2 + 6 + 10, then explain why these sums alone do not determine a ground-state configuration.

## luminara.adventure.001.q08

The gallery accumulates simulated electron-position results from many similarly prepared one-electron hydrogen atoms. A viewer says: “The dots are the orbit of one electron; all the dots are present as electrons in a single atom; and our uncertain F-or-Cl address is the same kind of quantum uncertainty.” Identify and repair all three claims without trying to derive quantum mechanics.

Progressive hints, optional:
1. Read how many atoms and preparations the display combines.
2. A probability distribution is not a connected trajectory or a changed electron inventory.
3. Missing identity information in the letter is not evidence of a quantum superposition.

## luminara.adventure.001.q09

Reference cards give neutral ground-state K as 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s¹ and Ca as 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s². Their 3d occupancy is zero. Explain why “fill every available n = 3 state before using any n = 4 state” fails. Distinguish permitted capacity from ground-state energy ordering. Keep your conclusion within these supplied examples.

Progressive hints, optional:
1. Count electrons and compare the written occupancies with the proposed instruction.
2. The existence of an empty 3d category does not invalidate the reference cards.
3. The lowest-energy allowed arrangement is not determined solely by putting principal numbers in ascending order.

## luminara.adventure.001.q10

Moseley's historical work used X-ray spectra to support an elemental order associated with nuclear charge. In the story, he visits the bus, receives a modern briefing, and helps analyze the letter. Separate the historical contribution, the later scientific explanation, and the invented events. Why can his expertise improve the question without making the original incomplete address uniquely identify chlorine?

Progressive hints, optional:
1. Scientific sources do not authenticate invented dialogue.
2. The 1913 visitor is explicitly given later proton/configuration language.
3. A more knowledgeable reader cannot infer an unprovided period from a pattern that fits both candidates.

## luminara.adventure.001.q13

A record says: neutral ground-state first-twenty atom, atomic number 17, period 2. Explain why this differs from the original two-candidate clue. State what can be concluded conditionally from each conflicting entry, and propose a useful response without silently deciding which entry was mistyped.

Progressive hints, optional:

## luminara.adventure.001.q14

Someone proposes 1s² 2s³ 2p⁴ for neutral ground-state fluorine. Count the electrons. Then use the maximum s-subshell capacity of two and the verified reference 1s² 2s² 2p⁵ to explain what is correct, what is invalid, and what should be repaired. Why would “keep checking your addition” be unhelpful feedback?

Progressive hints, optional:
1. The sum 2 + 3 + 4 has no arithmetic error.
2. An s subshell cannot accommodate three electrons in the orbital model.
3. Electron total, allowed occupancy, and correct ground-state arrangement are separate checks.

Return to [scene 18](STORY.md#ch01-scene-18).

## luminara.adventure.001.q15

The chapter supplies approximate first ionization energies of 17.4 eV for F and 13.0 eV for Cl. Both are neutral gaseous ground-state atoms for this comparison. Explain how these values test “all members of a group behave identically.” State what first ionization energy measures and two claims these values do not establish.

Progressive hints, optional:
1. A single well-defined property difference defeats an absolute identity claim.
2. This property concerns removing one electron, not adding one.
3. It is not a universal reaction rate, a complete reactivity ranking, or evidence that group patterns are useless.

Return to [scene 16](STORY.md#ch01-scene-16).

## luminara.adventure.001.q16

A repaired system can report (a) one supported candidate, (b) several surviving candidates, or (c) conflicting inputs or an invalid configuration. Choose one chapter example for each outcome and explain the next useful action. Then write one sentence showing why keeping uncertainty when evidence is incomplete does not require staying uncertain after distinguishing evidence arrives.

Progressive hints, optional:
1. Think of the original letter before and after period 3, then the conflicting period/atomic-number card.
2. Different unresolved states need different next steps.
3. Conclusions should change when relevant evidence changes; permanent hesitation is not the goal.
